## Page 30

Concrete > “Cool”

“Boring” industries can offer great projects

-  Don’t just focus on “hot” companies/industries Scope your assigned project around a specific change initiative

-  A broad, ill-defined, or upcoming initiative is hard to analyze in 15 pages

-  You MUST cover all 3 lenses in your analysis

-  You MUST have 2-3 substantive recommendations

Figure out if you need an NDA early-on

-  We will safely store the information you give us.

-  Can we speak freely with 10-15 employees?

-  All teams are required to work with the standard MIT NDA

## Page 31

Create a project plan

Don’t wait until the last minute

-  Coordinating with the company (and each of your schedules) takes time

Collect as much data on the change initiative as possible

-  Conduct interviews, sit in real/virtual meetings, analyze company documents, etc.

Prepare for interviews

-  Determine the best questions for each; triangulate where possible

-  Have an interview script but be ready to pivot if the opportunity arises.

Use TAs and your faculty advisor for help

-  Communicate challenges early. We are here to help!

## Page 32

You are NOT a hired consultant

You are observing and analyzing the company; not working for them

-  You are not there to do a marketing or strategy analysis or a survey

-  Beware of scope creep and ask for support where needed

Don’t be afraid to make bold recommendations

-  Only your faculty advisor will read your OP Team Project report

-  Don’t be afraid to critique honestly if it’s constructive

-  Make ambitious recommendations in the paper; you can scale them back in your host deliverable

## Page 33

Remember, you represent MIT Sloan

Relationship + network management is key

-  Define expectations early on. Communicate often. Follow through.

-  Do not ghost your host! They are expecting some kind of deliverable (e.g., expanded Comms presentation, executive summary document, etc.).

Remain courteous and professional at all times

-  Virtual meetings feel casual, but ensure you are still respecting the organization’s dress code when attending meetings or interviews.

Host organizations will provide feedback on your team at the end of the project
