Alignment: Key Considerations

- People care deeply about pay equity

- Me ≠ You → decreased motivation

- Reward ≠ Effort → decreased effort (e.g., “quiet quitting”)

- Pay for performance can be difficult to execute well

- Use SMARTE goals (i.e., specific, measurable, attainable, relevant, timely, equitable)

- Set goals at multiple levels to broaden scope of alignment

- Design systems with expectancy, instrumentality, and valence in mind

## Page 10

Feedback as an Alignment Mechanism

Performance evaluations and other forms of feedback:

- Help employees

- Understand how they are progressing against firm and individual goals

- Decide if they should change strategies

- Choose where to focus their efforts

- Help employers

- Manage promotions, pay, etc.

- Facilitate employee development

## Page 11

Barriers to Effective Feedback

- Sugar coating – people want to be nice
- But overly negative news should not be a surprise!
- Why people may not be on the same page:

Cognitive constraint Managers over-emphasize recent events

Information constraint Managers don’t see or even know all day-to-day obstacles employees face

Affective constraint Employees have selective memory, denial, and projection, which distort their view of performance

As a result, feedback can reduce motivation instead of improving it

## Page 12

How to Give Effective Feedback

Use the “SBI” framework to keep it from being personal.

As Managers...

Describe the situation. Provide specifics: When? Where? Who?

Keep notes on progress over time to avoid recency effects

As Managers...

Describe the observable behavior. Don’t assume you know what the other person was thinking.
