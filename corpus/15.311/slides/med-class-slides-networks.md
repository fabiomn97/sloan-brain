## Page 5

Good Networks for Hard Times

Among workers who are laid off from the same firm at the same time:

- 8% less time between jobs with a better network (Cingano & Rosolia, 2012)

- 2-3x more likely to be hired at a firm with a network tie (Saygin et al., 2019; Garcia-Louzao & Silva, 2023)

- Networks in your specific cohort in college are crucial (Ost et al., 2023)

In 2026, your network matters more than ever

- 70% of professionals use AI in their job search (Statista, 2026)

-  Employers are shifting weight to criteria that are harder to fake (Bloomberg, 2026)

## Page 13

Barrier #1: It feels instrumental

We see networking as “calculated” rather than genuine or authentic.

Build ties before you need them

I have a specific goal, and that feels transactional

Connect through shared activities

The interactions are often sterile or forced

Reframe as a commitment to learning

Knowing it’s important makes it feel like a chore

Networking stops feeling instrumental when the tie precedes the need.

## Page 14

Barrier #2: I have nothing to offer

We assume that networking is an exchange of resources

- Task-related (e.g., information, technical support, money)

- Position-related (e.g., visibility, reputation, contacts)

But, there is a broad range of “currencies” to exchange in

- Relationships: friendship, inclusion, understanding

- Status: deference, gratitude

- Inspiration: meaning, morality, mastery

## Page 15

Barrier #3: It won’t go well

We can have anxiety or skepticism about how the interaction will go.

Liking Gap

“They won’t like me...”

We underestimate how much others enjoy interacting with us (Boothby et al., 2018) Helping Gap

“They don’t want to help...”

We underestimate how likely others are to accept requests for help (Flynn & Lake, 2008)

Reach-Out Gap

“It’s been too long...”

We underestimate how much others appreciate reconnecting (Liu et al., 2023)

We tend to expect a worse outcome or reaction than we get in reality.
