MIT Sloan School of Management 15.060: Data, Models, and Decisions – Fall 2026

Course Syllabus

Instructors: Chara Podimata, Rama Ramakrishnan and Evan Yao Description

Analytics is changing how businesses and organizations operate. Decisions, regardless of industry or function, are increasingly becoming data-driven and model-driven. In this environment, it is imperative for Sloan MBA students – the executives and entrepreneurs of tomorrow - to become fluent in the concepts, methods, and tools of business analytics.

Accordingly, this subject introduces first-year Sloan students to the fundamental techniques of using data and analytics techniques to make informed management decisions. We will focus on various ways of modeling, or thinking structurally about, decision problems in order to enhance decision-making skills.

Rather than survey all the techniques of management science, we stress those fundamental concepts that we believe are most important for the practical analysis of management decisions. Consequently, we focus on imparting a pragmatic approach to exploring and understanding structured data, quantifying uncertainty explicitly and incorporating it systematically in decision-making, using historical data to build effective predictive models, simulating complex systems, and developing prescriptive models that can optimally allocate scarce resources. The implementation of these tools is facilitated considerably by software, and we will make liberal use of spreadsheet models and the R statistical analysis platform.

It is impossible to teach you all there is to know about management science and data science techniques in a single semester; rather, our goal is to enable you to become intelligent users of these techniques and discerning consumers of their output. In that vein, emphasis will be placed on how, what and why certain techniques and tools are useful, and what their ramifications would be when used in practice, all in concert with the overarching goal for you to become excellent managers.  This will necessitate some manipulations of formulas and data, but it is not our goal for you to become adept handlers of mathematical equations and computer software. Rather, we want to provide you with first-hand experience in the practice of analytics. This will give you a deeper level of understanding of analytics than just reading about it ever could and will serve you well in your career.

To give you a perspective on how management science and data science are used in practice, much of the material will be presented in the context of practical business situations from a variety of settings. We hope that this illustrative material will motivate you to continue your analytics journey at MIT in selecting future courses like The AI Edge and in pursuing the Business Analytics Certificate.

Attendance Policy

Students are expected to fully attend all classes. Attendance and participation enhance the quality of learning in the MBA Core through the sharing of lived experience and perspective of class participants.

An excused absence will be granted for religious observance, illness or emergency.  MBA Core semester faculty will also allow students up to 2 excused absences per course (1 for courses that meet only once per week) for pre-arranged and unavoidable professional development conflicts.

It is the responsibility of the student to notify their faculty or TAs of all absences.  Any request for an excused professional development absence should be sent in advance and briefly identify the nature of the conflict (e.g. name of the company or conference).

Absences beyond this will be considered unexcused and will impact a student’s final course grade.

Access to the class livestream/recording will only be granted for medical absences and religious observances (https://registrar.mit.edu/calendar/religious-holidays).

Students whose attendance is impacted by recurring illness/personal emergency should notify their faculty/TAs of absences immediately and meet with their program advisor to discuss.

Midterms and Final Exams in all Core semester courses are mandatory and excused absences will only be granted for illness or religious holiday observance.

Policy on the Use of AI tools

-  Policy: Students may use GenAI to support learning, brainstorming, editing, debugging, or generating explanations. Prohibited uses include: Copying full problem prompts into GenAI tools, asking GenAI to solve assigned problems, or submitting AI-generated text, code, equations, or analysis as one’s own.
-  Rationale: By using AI to complete assignments, you will not develop as independent and critical thinkers. Learning and growth require productive struggle, and AI, by removing the struggle, seriously reduces your learning.
-  Please note that the midterm and final exams will be held in class and you will not have access to AI tools. Academic Support Services

If you have a personal or medical issue that is impacting your ability to attend class, complete work, or take an exam, please discuss this with your program advisor along with notifying your instructors and TAs.  If you don’t know the name of your advisor, please contact your Program Office.

Please review the Student Resources tab on the course Canvas site for information on academic and personal support services and resources at MIT.
