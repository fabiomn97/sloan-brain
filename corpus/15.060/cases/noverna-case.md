Noverna1

Please read and discuss this mini-case with your core team members. The main question to be answered is whether it is more profitable for Noverna to pursue the development of the new drug compared to not pursuing it.

Noverna2 is a biotech firm located in Kendall square, Cambridge MA, focusing on the development of therapeutics and vaccines for novel viruses. The company currently has one commercial therapeutic, approved in 2015, used in the treatment of a type of coronavirus that still occasionally breaks-out in certain parts of the world. The projected future profits from this therapeutic are estimated to be $275 million3.

With the emergence and spread of a novel type of coronavirus around the globe, Noverna is considering dedicating and accelerating its research efforts towards the development of a treatment for this pandemic. The company has recently built significant expertise in a new type of therapeutic technology, based on messenger RNA, that can potentially lead to a vaccine against this new virus within a matter of a few months.

RNA (ribonucleic acid) is a molecule that plays an important role in gene expression. Cellular organisms use a type of RNA, called messenger RNA (mRNA), to covey genetic information to ribosomes where proteins are synthesized. The idea underlying mRNA-based therapeutics is to administer specific mRNA molecules into the human body with a coding sequence that causes the body to synthesize a protein that fights the disease or acts as a vaccine. This technology, if successful, can have vast implications in medicine, well beyond this pandemic.

Considerable uncertainty, however, exists regarding the efficacy and market potential of this new treatment. The drug efficacy can be “low” or “high”. If the drug generates substantial antibody response without adverse side effects then its efficacy will be considered high and it can be used as a vaccine against the virus. However, if the antibody response is low or if the adverse effects require a dose reduction (effectively lowering the antibody response) then the efficacy is low and the drug would not be effective as a vaccine. Instead, in the low efficacy scenario, it would be used as one of several treatments currently administered to virus patients to help reduce their symptoms or recovery time. The economic attractiveness of this treatment would be significantly lower than if it were a vaccine.

Noverna is aware that several other pharmaceutical companies are attempting to develop vaccines and other treatments for the novel coronavirus, using different and diverse technologies. This threatens not only the economic viability of Noverna’s new potential drug, but also its existing coronavirus treatment. Several scenarios are possible, but they can be grouped into two main ones: “low competition” and “high competition”. Estimating the probabilities associated with these scenarios is very difficult. However, Noverna believes its mRNA technology is the most likely one to succeed and believes the odds are in its favor that it would face low competition. The best current estimate Noverna has is a 70% probability that competition will be “low” and a 30% probability that the competition will be high. For ethical and economic reasons, if Noverna decides to pursue the R&D of its new treatment then it would want to do so immediately without waiting to further assess the competition landscape. And it would be too late to launch the product after competitors have launched theirs. The premium of being the first treatment in the market is significant.

The economic consequences of Noverna’s decision are very substantial and vary widely depending of the efficacy and competition scenarios. The decision could very well determine the company’s future.
