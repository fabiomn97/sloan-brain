## Page 16

Module 1: Uncertainty

- Example: Whole Foods must decide how many cartons of blueberries to stock each day. o Too many: leftover blueberries go to waste => lower margins o Too few: customers cannot buy blueberries => lost sales, long-term impact on

customer loyalty

- Blueberry demand is impossible to predict exactly and has inherent variability from day to day.

- Using historic data, we model the variability of blueberry demand using a distribution and make a decision that is good on average.

## Page 17

Module 2: Prediction

-  In module 2, we make individual-level decisions that are hard because the relationship between variables is complex.

-  Example: Whole Foods wants to predict whether a specific shopper is about to churn (i.e., shop somewhere else) to decide who to send a retention coupon to. o Miss a churner → lose the customer for good

o Flag a loyal shopper → waste a retention offer

-  Churn has no single cause — it depends on many interacting signals: visit frequency, basket size, time since last visit, shopper gender, shopper age…etc.

-  Using historic data, we build models like Linear and Logistic Regression that can learn these complex patterns from historical data, i.e., what separates churners from loyal shoppers. We use that model to score each shopper individually.

## Page 18

Module 3: Optimization

-  In module 3, we make many inter-related decisions (coupled by complex business constraints) holistically

-  Example: Whole Foods must route its delivery trucks from the distribution center to restock its stores today. The orders are already placed and fixed. o Each truck can carry a limited load. Some trucks have coolers some do not. o Each store has a delivery window. o We want to design routes and assign them to trucks so as to achieve the least total delivery cost

-  The challenge lies is the staggering number of ways to split stores across routes, assign to trucks and sequence the stops while satisfying every constraint

-  We write this as an optimization model — decision variables, an objective, and constraints — and use Excel/Python to search for the cheapest feasible plan
