## Page 81

Recall a common consulting interview problem…

A common consulting interview problem involves estimating some quantity (often sizing a market) through reasonable assumptions

How many cups of coffee are consumed in Cambridge each day? How many golf balls can fit inside a Boeing 747?

It is too hard to estimate the quantity outright, so the right approach is to:

1. Model the quantity of interest as a computation involving simpler quantities 2. Estimate each of those simpler quantities using prior domain knowledge or limited available data 3. Compute your final estimate by running a calculation using these simple estimates

## Page 82

How many cups of coffee are consumed in Cambridge each day?

1. Model the quantity of interest as a computation involving simpler quantities

o Population of Cambridge * % of people who drink coffee each day * Average drinks per day for

coffee-drinkers

2. Estimate each of those simpler quantities with a number based on prior domain knowledge or limited available data

o Population of Cambridge: 100,000

o % of people who drink coffee each day: 40% o Average drinks per day for coffee-drinkers: 1.25

3. Compute your final estimate by running a calculation using these simple estimates

o 100,000 * 40% * 1.25 = 50,000 cups of coffee

Consulting Market Sizing Problem

Probabilistic Modeling

- Model the quantity of interest as a computation involving simpler quantities

- Model the outcome random variable as a computation involving simpler random variables

- Estimate each of those simpler quantities with a number based prior domain knowledge or limited available data

- Estimate the distribution of each of those simpler random variables using domain knowledge or limited research ability.

- Compute your final estimate by running a calculation

- Calculate your final outcome’s distribution by running a simulation

What is Monte-Carlo Simulation

- A Monte-Carlo Simulation generates synthetic data by running the probabilistic model.

- It typically involves many tens of thousands of scenarios or trials. In each scenario, we:

1. Sample one value from each input distribution.

2. Compute every output using the model's formulas. 3. Record the outputs from that trial and create a histogram at the end
