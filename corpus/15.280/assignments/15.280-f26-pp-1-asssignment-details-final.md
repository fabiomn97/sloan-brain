15.280 PERSUASIVE PRESENTATION 1

Assignment Details

This assignment includes three deliverables and constitutes 20% of your final 15.280 grade:

1) A 4-minute Persuasive Presentation + 2 min of Q&A scheduled outside of class time; 2) A Planner + Shield due at the time of your presentation; 3) A Self-Assessment of your Presentation due 48 hours after your presentation.

PERSUASIVE PRESENTATION + Q&A Task: Create and deliver an appropriately scoped persuasive presentation to a defined, specific audience that convinces them to accept a specific recommendation or take a specific course of action. You will create original content for this assignment that draws upon your professional interests. Previous content created for work or school is not acceptable. You must choose a non-MIT Sloan audience to ensure solid thinking about ‘what’s in it for them’ (WIIFT). Topic selection is important so if you have questions and/or are considering a topic related to a start-up idea, consult with your professor in advance of your presentation to determine if it aligns with the assignment. Objective: You will determine a specific and measurable communication objective, i.e. what do you want your audience TO DO because of your presentation. Sample objectives include:

-  Persuade your company's management to pilot a company-wide community service day.
-  Persuade your company's HR team to add personal finance training to new employee onboarding.
-  Persuade your manager to start a weekly "lunch and learn" program with other teams. Timing: You have 4 uninterrupted minutes for your formal remarks followed by an additional 2 minutes to field questions from your audience for a total time of 6 minutes. Prior to the start of your presentation, you will have one minute to set up your audience. This means providing those in your presentation session with the key information they will need to effectively take on their audience roles. This can include their job titles, their biases, and company background. Additional Situational Guidance:  Given the time constraints, it’s likely your presentation will take place during a meeting with other items on the agenda. Keep this situational guidance in mind as you plan the different elements of your presentation. Check with us if you have questions. Visual aids: No visual aids, i.e. PowerPoint, props, notes, etc. are allowed in this presentation.

Date & location: Presentations are held outside of Class & Comm Lab; the sign-up process will be shared by your Professor and TA. PRESENTATION PLANNER

The Presentation Planner + Shield Framework are designed to help you think strategically about how you build a persuasive, logical and benefit-driven argument supported by concrete evidence and deliver it effectively to your target audience. Your Professor and TA will refer to your Planner + Shield as they grade your presentation. The quality of both is factored into your overall grade for this assignment; failure to submit them will lower your assignment grade. Both are due at the time of your presentation. AUDIENCE FEEDBACK

You will receive audience feedback from peers who attend your presentation session and serve as your audience. This feedback will evaluate your presentation based on how it can be more clear, credible, and convincing. You will learn more about this feedback in class and during your presentation session.

SELF-ASSESSMENT

You will watch the recording of your presentation, assess the strength of your persuasive argument and delivery, and next steps for improvement. To best assess your presentation, watch your recording at least twice. If you want to isolate your nonverbal delivery, watch the video only (with sound off). If you want to isolate your verbal delivery, watch without the video (sound only). Video and sound on give you the full effect and provide the most effective way to gauge your overall persuasiveness. Your Self-Assessment should be specific, credible, and thorough, and address each grading rubric category below. For each category, you should identify at least two “strengths” and two “areas for improvement” with supporting details and specific moments from your presentation cited. No more than 1 page. The quality of your Self-Assessment is factored into your overall grade for this assignment. Failure to submit within 48 hours of your presentation will lower your assignment grade. GRADING RUBRIC

These criteria reflect five key elements that contribute to an effective, persuasive presentation. Your presentation will be evaluated on whether it meets, exceeds, or falls below the following criteria: STRATEGY:

1. There is a specific and measurable communication objective/recommendation, i.e., what you want the audience TO DO because of your presentation, which is appropriately scoped for the time allotted. 2. There is one core message that is clear, concise, and memorable. 3. The presentation is persuasive and establishes WIIFT STRUCTURE:

1. Direct or indirect structure and one- or two-sided argument are correctly chosen and executed based on an accurate analysis of the situation and audience. 2. Introduction attracts attention, sets appropriate context, and gives a preview of presentation; conclusion reiterates core message and communicates a call-to-action for the audience. 3. Transitions between points ensure presentation is easy to follow and, as necessary, include signposting. EVIDENCE:

1. The cited evidence persuades the audience to accept your recommendation. 2. The cited evidence is accurate, credible, current, and aligned with supporting points. 3. The cited evidence is sourced.

Q&A MANAGEMENT:

1. Guidelines for audience participation, i.e. when they can ask questions, are set at the start of the presentation. 2. Noticeable effort to build audience rapport during Q&A, e.g. active listening, paraphrasing. 3. Questions are answered credibly and reinforce main message.

DELIVERY:

1. Verbal delivery is dynamic and engaging; this includes variation in tone, pace, volume, and absence of filler words. 2. Non-verbal delivery displays confidence and presence; this includes effective eye contact, open posture, purposeful movement, absence of fidgeting or other distracting behaviors. 3. Timing, you spend the appropriate amount of time on the critical aspects of the argument and do not exceed four minutes for your formal remarks or two minutes for Q&A.
