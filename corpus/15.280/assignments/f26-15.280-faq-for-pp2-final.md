FAQ: Persuasive Presentation 2

Here are some common questions asked about Persuasive Presentation 2 with answers that clarify how you should build on Persuasive Presentation 1 and what you should expect. Can I reuse content from Persuasive Presentation 1 for Persuasive Presentation 2? Persuasive Presentation 2 should represent an evolution, advancement and/or refinement of the communication strategy you used for Persuasive Presentation 1. Imagine that first presentation secured a second meeting to discuss your recommendation in more detail. So, you should think critically about what worked well and what didn’t. You should also think critically about the ways your argument may need to change under closer scrutiny and questioning. You may need to strengthen your evidence, highlight your credibility more, show deeper knowledge of your topic and/or restructure supporting points for greater impact. While you can reuse content from your first presentation, PP2 should be an enhancement of PP1. How will I know what to keep from Persuasive Presentation 1 and what to change? The most effective way to prepare for Persuasive Presentation 2 is to review your Self-Assessment for Persuasive Presentation 1 along with the feedback you received from your professor or TA. Both the Self- Assessment and feedback identified “strengths” and “areas for improvement” for Strategy, Structure, Evidence, Delivery and Q&A Management. Build on the strengths and consider ways to address the areas for improvement. Also, after your first presentation, you received audience feedback from those in your presentation session. That feedback focused on how you could make your presentation more clear, credible and convincing. Based on that feedback, consider how to make your recommendation more clear, credible and convincing and, generally, how to sharpen your argument for your specific audience. Should I have a different audience for Persuasive Presentation 2? As your recommendation gets more serious consideration in a second meeting, it’s very likely you will be presenting in front of a slightly different audience. That audience may include higher ranking executives and/or executives who will be most directly impacted by your recommendation. You should think critically about their information needs. You also should think critically about how your WIIFT and persuasive tactics may need to change in consideration of new audience members. How much content should I prepare for an 8-minute “interactive” presentation? As a general rule, you should prepare about 5-6 minutes of content and expect to use 2-3 minutes to answer questions that will interrupt you and come throughout the presentation. If you receive a high number of questions, you may not get to all your content. Think ahead about how you will prioritize content if you cannot present all that you have prepared. Can I defer questions until the end? No, you must manage questions in the moment. And/or at a minimum, you should acknowledge questions, provide a partial answer, and indicate when you will give additional info later in your presentation. Be prepared to be interrupted. Also, be prepared for questions that may pull you away from your planned structure. Think ahead about how you will cover essential content with a logical flow when questions naturally lead to material you plan to address later.
