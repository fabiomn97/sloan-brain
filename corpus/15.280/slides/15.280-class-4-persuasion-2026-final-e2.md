## Page 11

To be an effective persuader, you need options

-  Aristotle once defined rhetoric as “the process of finding the available means of persuasion”

-  Your challenge: acquire a range of options to choose from depending on the objective and audience

-  The most effective persuaders consistently choose the appropriate strategies and tactics relative to their audience

Source: M. Jimmie Killingsworth, Appeals in Modern Rhetoric: An Ordinary Language Approach (Carbondale, IL: Southern Illinois Press, 2005), 153. 12

## Page 18

Reframe as a benefit: example

“We should implement this proposed initiative.”

”Implementing the proposed initiative will be good for the company.”

Framed as a benefit for audience (Manager responsible for P&L):

”Implementing the proposed initiative will allow your team to exceed it’s profitability goals by year end.”

## Page 29

Reciprocity

People repay in kind

Tactics

- Gift giving

- Being helpful to others

- The golden rule

- Give and Take

Source: Robert Cialdini, “Harnessing the Science of Persuasion,” Harvard Business Review, October 2001.

Social Proof / Consensus

Tactics

- Leverage

credibility of others

- Highlight other

depts/orgs

Source: Robert Cialdini, “Harnessing the Science of Persuasion,” Harvard Business Review

Consistency

Tactics

public, and voluntary commitments

- First step, pilot projects

Tactics

Scarcity

benefits
