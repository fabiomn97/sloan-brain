A · THE PROJECT

What should exist at the end? One or two sentences. Try: “A team should build ___ that lets ___ do ___.”

A team should build an AI Advising Tool that lets MIT Sloan students chat through academic scenarios and goals and get back personalized course recommendations synthesized from MIT course information, Sloan degree and certificate requirements, Sloan data, co-curricular offerings, and advisor institutional knowledge, making Academic Advising conversations more informed and higher-impact.

## Page 2

What happens today, and why isn’t it working? If something already exists — a spreadsheet, a paper case, a simulation from 1998 — say what it is and where it falls short.

Two internal prototypes exist and are being iterated on: the MIT Course Agent https://mit-courseagent-production-70de.up.railway.app/ and MIT Sloan's own Course Planner https://mysloan.mit.edu/course-planner. Beyond these, students largely fend for themselves — building their own tools, using ChatGPT (which lacks current Sloan-specific data and often hallucinates prerequisites), or crowdsourcing advice informally from peers. Two separate prototypes exist, each with only part of what's needed — which is itself the problem. We don't need two models; we need one that combines the best, highest-value functionality of both:

-  Student journey status — the Course Planner tracks where a student is in their degree progress; the Course Agent doesn't, so its recommendations aren't grounded in what a student has actually completed or still needs
-  Course behavior in previous semesters — neither tool surfaces whether a course closed, ran with a large waitlist, or was undersubscribed in prior terms, so students can't gauge whether a course is a safe bet to plan around
-  Clear learning objectives to suggest real paths — faculty often don't upload syllabi, so neither tool can ground recommendations in what a course actually teaches, only in its title and description
-  Fragmented ownership — requirements, co-curriculars, and advisor knowledge sit in separate systems, so even a well-informed conversation can't draw on all of it at once The result: two partial tools instead of one that combines the Course Planner's degree-progress awareness, the Course Agent's conversational advisor layer, and the historical/behavioral course data neither currently has.

Who is the user? Who will actually use this, roughly how many of them, and how often. “Just me” is a fine answer.

The users will be Sloan students from all programs and Sloan academic advisors.

B · SCOPE

Must-haves Two to four bullets: what does the smallest genuinely useful version have to do?

-  Course Selection – Help students select courses based upon interest area and skill development
-  Student Aware – Be able to give advice based upon student degree/program/certificate and their semester/year in the program
-  Course Scheduling – Help students create a tentative or actual course schedule for the current semester and future semester
-  Conversational Functionality – to allow for scenario playing and advising guidance

E · AFTER THE SEMESTER

If it works, what happens to it? Who owns and maintains it, whether it would really be used, whether there is budget for hosting or subscriptions, and anything on credit, IP, or confidentiality — students document their work in a public portfolio and class gallery.

It will be owned by Sloan/Sloan Technology Services.

Optional — the pitch Every year, Sloan students say the same thing: "I want to know about that class, that club, that faculty, that lab, that research assistant or TA opportunity, that path, that will allow me to attain my goals." We're building an AI Advising Agent that makes that possible. At any point in a student's journey, it answers the question: "Given where I am, where I want to go, and what I'm missing, what should I be doing right now?" It fuses what today lives in two disconnected prototypes, degree progress tracking and conversational, advisor style recommendations, with what neither has: real course behavior (waitlists, closures, prior term patterns) and real learning objectives (not just course titles, but what a course actually teaches). The result is a single tool that turns a student's background and target role or goal into a clear, personalized path, your next move, grounded in your history and pointed straight at your destination.
