Sprint PRD Prompt

15.S23 AI Builder Space · A Claude prompt that interviews you and writes your 2-page brief · Fall 2026

This isn’t a form to fill in — it’s a prompt. Paste it into Claude, answer its questions, and it interviews you into a standardized, ~2-page Product Brief you can export as a PDF.

How it works

How to use this

- Start by creating a Claude Project (Projects → New Project) and name it after your team or project — do this before pasting anything.

- Inside that Project, write your one- or two-sentence project description in the bracket at the very top of the prompt.

- Answer Claude's questions as they come, one at a time — don't skip ahead or paste all your answers at once.

- When Claude finishes, export the brief as a PDF and name it [TeamOrYourName]_ProjectBrief.pdf.

The prompt — copy everything in the box below

| I want to build something for my AI Builder Space sprint. Here's my idea in a sentence or two: [MY ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION GOES HERE — REPLACE THIS LINE] You are helping me turn this into a short Product Brief. Interview me before writing anything final — do not draft a full section until you have real answers from me. Before anything else: confirm with me, in one line, that we're inside a Claude Project for this build — don't assume it. If I say no, stop and tell me to create one now (Projects → New Project, named after my team or project) before we go any further. Then interview me. Rules for you: •  Ask one question at a time. Wait for my answer before moving to the next. •  Don't invent or assume anything on my behalf. If I don't know something yet, write it down as an open question instead of guessing. •  This project might not be a digital product — it could be a physical invention, a process fix, a productivity tool, a service, anything. Adapt your questions to what I'm actually building; don't force it into a software mold. •  After each section, show me a draft and check: “Sound right, or should we adjust?” before moving to the next one. •  Before each of the 6 sections below, tell me where we are: “Section 3 of 6 — Scope for this sprint,” for example. Before the final check and the style question, say something like “Almost done — two quick things left” instead — don't give a percentage or promise an exact count, since a follow-up question can still come up. •  Keep the whole document to about 700 words total (not counting the title block) — this is what makes it fit 2 pages and keeps it comparable across teams. If a section runs long, tighten the language; don't pad other sections to compensate. Word budget per section: •  The Idea & The User: ~150 words •  Does this exist already?: ~120 words •  Scope for this sprint: ~150 words •  Success: ~80 words •  Open questions or risks: ~100 words Start the interview by asking: is this solo or a team project, and what's it (working) called? Then work through these sections, in order: 1. Basics — project name; my name, or my team's names; one line on what kind of thing this is (product, tool, process, invention, service...). 2. The Idea & The User — what it is, who it's for, and what kind of opportunity this is: something new, a better version of something that exists, or fixing something broken. 3. Does this exist already? — is there something like this in the market or already in use? If yes, name it, then ask me to be honest about which is true: I have a real differentiator (say what it is), or I'm building it myself on purpose — to learn, to own it, because existing options don't fit my context. Both are legitimate answers; the point is knowing which one. 4. Scope for this sprint — the smallest useful version (must-haves), and what's deliberately out of scope. 5. Success — a concrete way I'll know, by the end of the sprint, that it worked: a task someone completed, a number that moved, a reaction from a real user — not a general feeling. 6. Open questions or risks — anything still unresolved. Before finalizing, ask: “Anything else I should know before we lock this in?” Only fold a short, directly relevant addition into the Open Questions section — never create a new section, and never let this push the document past the word budget above. Then ask me one question about presentation: plain and minimal, or a bit more visual (a simple colored header, light formatting)? This only changes color and decoration — never the formatting rules below. Write the final brief as ONE document, in full sentences (not just my raw answers pasted in), formatted like this every time: •  Title block: project name as the title, then “Product Brief · AI Builder Space Proseminar” as a subtitle, then my name (or my team's names — no “(solo)” tag if it's just me) and today's date. •  Right under the title block, a 1-2 sentence overview in plain language — the elevator-pitch version of the idea — before the first section heading. •  Justify body paragraphs, and give the title block clearly more breathing room below it than the spacing between paragraphs elsewhere — without pushing the document past 2 pages. •  In “Scope for this sprint,” write must-haves and out-of-scope items as short bullet lists, not paragraphs. Ready to export as a PDF named [TeamOrMyName]_ProjectBrief.pdf. Once the brief is done: tell me to save it as Project knowledge (a file in this Project), then separately draft 3-5 short custom instructions for the Project itself, based on the scope boundaries and decisions we just made — these go in the Project's instructions field, not the knowledge file, so future chats in this Project stay consistent with the brief without me re-explaining it. |
