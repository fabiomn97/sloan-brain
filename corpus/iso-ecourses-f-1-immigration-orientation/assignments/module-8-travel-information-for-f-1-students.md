**Module 8: Travel Information For F-1 Students**

**REQUIRED: A student must confirm that they *"have read and understand all the information within this module”* by marking this module as "DONE" at the bottom of the page.**

**U.S. Entry Information**

Individuals entering the U.S. in F status (F-1 or F-2) are ***REQUIRED*** to carry and present the following immigration documents (https://iso.mit.edu/visas-and-travel/entering-the-u-s/) at a U.S. port of entry.

- ***DO NOT*** pack these documents in checked baggage. They ***MUST*** be kept in carry-on luggage and easily accessible during travel.

|   |

**Requirements for Entry into the U.S.**

***Initial Entry Requirements BEFORE Form I-20 Start Date***

New, incoming, or transfer-in F-1 international students, along with any F-2 dependents, may enter the U.S. ***no earlier*** ***than 30 days before*** the program start date listed on their MIT-issued Form I-20.

The following documents (https://iso.mit.edu/visas-and-travel/entering-the-u-s/entering-the-us-borders-as-a-first-time-student/) ***MUST*** be carried in hand and presented to U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP (https://www.cbp.gov/travel/international-visitors/applying-admission-united-states)) upon arrival:

- **1) Valid Passport**: A passport ***MUST*** be valid for at least six (6) months into the future ***at all times***.
- **2) Valid F-1 Visa Sticker/Stamp**: An F-1 entry visa sticker/stamp ***MUST*** be valid and unexpired to enter the U.S. **NOTE**: *Not required for citizens of Canada and Bermuda*.
- **3) “Certificate of Eligibility” Immigration Document(s)**: The MIT Form I-20 ***MUST*** be printed and physically signed (with a wet signature) by the student. **NOTE**: U.S. regulations (https://www.ice.gov/doclib/sevis/pdf/I20-guidance.pdf) ***prohibit*** electronic signatures on Form I-20s by students.
- **4) Evidence of SEVIS Fee Payment**: A printed copy of the SEVIS fee payment confirmation receipt.
- **5) Proof of Admission to MIT**: A printed copy of the official MIT acceptance letter.
- **6) Evidence of Financial Support**: Financial documentation matching the information listed on the MIT Form I-20. Refer to the ISO website [here (https://iso.mit.edu/getting-started/requesting-an-i-20-or-ds-2019/financial-documentation-requirements/)] for details on the acceptable financial documents.

***Additional Travel Guidance***

- Students transiting through or entering a country other than their home country of citizenship or permanent residence ***MUST*** verify entry and transit requirements in advance.

Students should verify the relevant information for the country or countries in question. A directory of foreign embassies/consulates is available on the U.S. Department of State website [here (https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/consularnotification/ConsularNotificationandAccess.html)].

|   |

**U.S. Entry Requirements *AFTER* Initial Entry, & *BEFORE* Form I-20 Start Date**

***[NOT RECOMMENDED (https://iso.mit.edu/visas-and-travel/entering-the-u-s/entering-the-us-borders-as-a-first-time-student/#TravelAfterArrival)]***

Although departing the U.S. is ***never restricted***, returning ***AFTER*** the initial entry in F status, and ***BEFORE*** the program start date listed on the Form I-20, creates a high-risk situation (https://iso.mit.edu/visas-and-travel/entering-the-u-s/entering-the-us-borders-as-a-first-time-student/#TravelAfterArrival).

- After entering the U.S. in F status for the first time, one is ***REQUIRED*** to meet ***ALL*** re-entry requirements (https://iso.mit.edu/visas-and-travel/entering-the-u-s/entering-the-u-s-continuing-f-1-students/) for a “Continuing F-1 Student.”
- Once an individual enters the U.S. in F status, ***they are expected to maintain that status continuously for ALL* *future re-entries*** until they complete their MIT degree program or any authorized practical training.

****Example Scenario***

- Tim is an international student whose program at MIT will begin on September 3.
- Tim entered the U.S. in F-1 status on August 7.
- Tim left the U.S. for a short trip to Greenland on August 15.
- Tim plans to return to the U.S. on August 27, which is before his program’s start date (September 3).

***Since Tim has already made an initial entry in F-1 status, he *MUST* now meet the re-entry requirements (https://iso.mit.edu/visas-and-travel/entering-the-u-s/entering-the-u-s-continuing-f-1-students/) for a "Continuing F-1 Student". However, Tim *DOES NOT* meet the criteria because the following two (2) conditions have *NOT* been met:**

- 1) Tim’s SEVIS record and F-1 status have ***NOT*** been activated.
  - **NOTE**: SEVIS activation can ***ONLY*** occur after classes begin and the student is officially enrolled.

- 2) Tim’s Form I-20 has ***NOT*** been signed for travel.
  - **NOTE**: Travel signatures can ***ONLY*** be issued after the program start date on the Form I-20 and the student has completed all ***REQUIRED*** arrival and check-in procedures (https://iso.mit.edu/getting-started/arrival-at-mit-checklist/).

- Since neither condition can be fulfilled before the program start date, Tim ***CANNOT*** re-enter the U.S. in F status on August 27.

***Critical Travel Guidance***

- Due to these restrictions, traveling outside the U.S. after initial entry in F status and returning before the Form I-20 program start date is ***STRONGLY DISCOURAGED***. **The ISO *CANNOT* guarantee successful re-entry in these situations**.
  - Students should plan and consult with an ISO Advisor (https://iso.mit.edu/about-iso/student-advisors-by-program/) ***BEFORE*** making any travel plans that may involve leaving and re-entering the U.S. prior to the Form I-20 start date.

- If travel before the program start date is unavoidable, students may consider entering the U.S. in tourist/visitor status (B-2 or ESTA), exiting, and then re-entering in F-1 status closer to or after the program start date.
- **NOTE**: Students entering in tourist/visitor status are ***NOT*** permitted to study or complete the MIT check-in steps (https://iso.mit.edu/getting-started/online-check-in/) until they enter the U.S. in F status.

**U.S. Entry Requirements *AFTER* Initial Entry & Form I-20 Start Date**

Students entering the U.S. after their initial entry and Form I-20start date ***MUST*** carry and present the following documents (https://iso.mit.edu/visas-and-travel/entering-the-u-s/entering-the-u-s-continuing-f-1-students/) at the U.S. port of entry:

- **1) Valid Passport**: A passport ***MUST*** be valid for at least six (6) months into the future ***at all times***.
- **2) Valid F-1 Visa Sticker/Stamp**: An F-1 entry visa sticker/stamp ***MUST*** be valid and unexpired to enter the U.S. **NOTE**: *Not required for citizens of Canada and Bermuda*.
- **3) “Certificate of Eligibility” Form I-20 Document(s)**: The most recently issued Form I-20 that has been signed for travel (page 2) within the past 12 months.
  - ***Example**: If a Form I-20 was signed for travel on September 15, 2025, it remains valid for ***multiple trips*** until September 14, 2026 ***– OR –*** until the end date listed on page 1, ***whichever date comes first***.

- **4) Evidence of SEVIS Fee Payment**: A printed copy of the SEVIS fee payment confirmation receipt.
- **5) Evidence of Enrollment**: Can be obtained online (https://registrar.mit.edu/transcripts-records/transcripts-certifications-letters/enrollment-certifications) from the Registrar’s Office.
- **6) Evidence of Financial Support**: Financial documentation matching the information listed on the MIT Form I-20. Refer to the ISO website [here (https://iso.mit.edu/getting-started/requesting-an-i-20-or-ds-2019/financial-documentation-requirements/)] for details on the acceptable financial documents.

****How to Request an Updated Form I-20 with a Travel Signature***

Students can ***ONLY*** request (https://iso.mit.edu/visas-and-travel/entering-the-u-s/#ReqTravelSig) an updated Form I-20 with a “Travel Signature” from the ISO ***AFTER*** they have completed the following requirements:

- 1) The ISO *Immigration Orientation eCourse (https://iso.mit.edu/getting-started/orientation-program/)*.
- 2) The *Online Immigration ‘Check-In’* requirements (https://iso.mit.edu/getting-started/online-check-in/).
- 3) The student has registered for classes, and it is ***AFTER*** the start date listed on their immigration document (Form I-20 or Form DS-2019).

***Additional Travel Guidance***

- Students transiting through or entering a country other than their home country of citizenship or permanent residence ***MUST*** verify entry and transit requirements in advance.
  - Students should verify the relevant information for the country or countries in question. A directory of foreign embassies/consulates is available on the U.S. Department of State website [here (https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/consularnotification/ConsularNotificationandAccess.html)].

- **ATTENTION****: Individuals in F status should *NEVER(!!)* enter the U.S. using a B-1/B-2 tourist visa, ESTA, Visa Waiver Program (VWP), or any other non-student immigration status.**
  - Entering the U.S. in any status other than F will result in the ***termination*** of the F status (F-1 and F-2s) and the loss of legal eligibility to study in the U.S.

**IMPORTANT****: Travel & Document Reminders**

- ***Travel Signatures for New/Incoming Students***
  - New, incoming, or transfer-in students may only request a travel signature from the ISO ***AFTER*** the program start date listed on their Form I-20 and the completion of their post-arrival immigration requirements (https://iso.mit.edu/getting-started/arrival-at-mit-checklist/).

- ***Exiting the U.S.***
  - A valid travel signature on page 2 of a Form I-20 is ***NEVER*** required to exit the U.S.
    - ***A valid travel signature is ONLY REQUIRED*** ***for entry into the U.S.***

- ***Entering the U.S.***
  - Students ***MUST*** carry all ***required immigration documents (https://iso.mit.edu/visas-and-travel/entering-the-u-s/)*** when entering the U.S.
  - ***NEVER*** enter the U.S. in any status other than F-1 (e.g., B-1/B-2, ESTA, or Visa Waiver). Doing so will result in the loss of F-1 status and will ***require immediate departure*** from the U.S. and the issuance of a new immigration record.

- ***Travel Within the U.S.***
  - Students should keep digital copies of all their immigration documents (e.g., saved securely on a phone or cloud storage). Guidance varies based on travel location:
    - **On MIT Campus**: ***DO NOT*** carry original immigration documents. Keep them stored securely at home. Digital copies should be readily accessible.
    - **Outside MIT Campus**: Carry all original immigration documents (https://iso.mit.edu/news/new-advice-when-inside-the-u-s-please-carry-all-immigration-documents-travel-reminders-address-updates/) when not on campus at MIT.
      - **NOTE**: Students will need to show their passport as a valid form of identification in the U.S., if they do not have a “Massachusetts State ID (https://iso.mit.edu/how_to_guide/obtain-a-state-id/)” or “Massachusetts Driver's License (https://iso.mit.edu/how_to_guide/obtain-a-us-drivers-license/)”.

- ***ISO Processing Times***
  - Requests for new immigration documents are processed within ***10 business days***, starting from the date all required materials are submitted.
    - **NOTE**: We encourage students to be considerate of their peers and the ISO staff by ***remembering to check*** their immigration documents and requirements when arranging travel outside the U.S. This will ensure that requests can be made within a reasonable timeframe and processed equitably for all students.

- ***Delivery of Immigration Documents and Information***
  - Students can always access their official immigration documents, issued by the ISO, electronically in the *Document Viewer* section of their iMIT Homepage. Instructions on how to access these documents can be found on the ISO website [here (https://iso.mit.edu/knowledge-base/how-to-access-my-imit-documents/)].
  - **NOTE**: The ISO will always send official communications to a student’s @mit.edu email address.
