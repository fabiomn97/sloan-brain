**IMPORTANT REQUIREMENTS**

***Required Steps to Activate U.S. Immigration Status***

- All new/incoming international students (https://iso.mit.edu/getting-started/orientation-program/#WhoNewStu) ***MUST*** complete the following steps for the International Students Office (ISO) to activate their U.S. immigration status with both MIT and the U.S. government.

|   |

**STEP 1: I****mmigration Orientation eCourse (https://iso.mit.edu/getting-started/orientation-program/) Requirement**

- Complete this *F-1 Immigration Orientation eCourse (https://iso.mit.edu/getting-started/orientation-program/) for New/Incoming International Students*.
  - All F-1 students are ***REQUIRED*** to complete the Immigration Orientation eCourse.
  - A minimum score of 90% on the associated quiz is required to pass the eCourse.
    - **NOTE**: Students ***MUST*** save or take a screenshot of their quiz results. This documentation will be required for submission during Step 2 (*Required Immigration Online Check-In (https://iso.mit.edu/getting-started/online-check-in/)*).

- **IMPORTANT: This step** ***MUST*** **be completed *BEFORE* proceeding to the next step**.

|   |

**STEP 2: Immigration Online Check-In (https://iso.mit.edu/getting-started/online-check-in/) Requirement**

***Complete the REQUIRED*** ***Immigration Online Check-In Process (https://iso.mit.edu/getting-started/online-check-in/)***

- This step can ***ONLY*** be completed ***AFTER***:
  - A student has entered the U.S. in F-1 status.
  - The student has completed the Immigration Orientation eCourse.
  - Is within 30 days of the program start date listed on their Form I-20 (Certificate of Eligibility).

- The Immigration Online Check-In confirms a student’s arrival in the U.S. and is ***REQUIRED*** for activating their U.S. immigration status.

***Instructions for Completing the Immigration Online Check-In:***

**NOTE**: Students are ***REQUIRED*** to carefully review the ISO *Online Check-In* webpage (https://iso.mit.edu/getting-started/online-check-in/) (especially the section (https://iso.mit.edu/getting-started/online-check-in/#Tips) titled *‘Tips for Completing the Online Check-In Process’,* which includes detailed instructions and visual examples), and the following instructions:

- 1) Log in to iMIT (https://imit.mit.edu/) by selecting ‘*Full Client Services for Students*’.

- 2) In the left-hand menu, select the ‘*Upon Arrival*’ tab.

- 3) Choose the appropriate Online Check-In e-Form based on the program start term and year.
  - **Example**: A student beginning a program in Fall 2025 must select the ‘*Fall 2025 Check-In*’ e-Form.
  - If the correct term e-Form is ***NOT*** available (e.g., a Fall 2025 student only sees Summer 2025), the student ***MUST*** wait until the correct e-Form is available ***BEFORE*** continuing.

- 4) Select *‘Start New Request’* and complete ***ALL*** of the Immigration Online Check-In e-Forms, including:

-
  - **1) Student Status Section**
    - Complete the “Student Status” e-Form.

  - **2) Student Category Section**
    - Complete the “New/Transfer Students” e-Form.

  - **3) Online Check-In Section**
    - Complete ***ALL*** seven (7) e-Forms below, which will ***REQUIRE*** students to submit the following (https://iso.mit.edu/getting-started/online-check-in/#Tips) information and documentation:
      - **“1. Your Information”**
        -
          - Must confirm the information on file and also provide:
            - A U.S. phone number.
            - An Emergency contact.
            - A U.S. contact (if applicable).

      - **“2. Passport Information”**
        -
          - Provide a copy of a recently issued passport, if one has not already been provided to the ISO.

      - **“3. Visa Stamp Information and Upload”**
        -
          - Provide a copy of an F-1 visa sticker/stamp (except for citizens of Canada and Bermuda).
          - **NOTE**: It is acceptable if this document is expired or lists another school/university.

      - **“4. Local U.S. Address Information”**
        -
          - Provide a U.S. residential address to the ISO and in WebSIS (https://student.mit.edu/sfprwhom.html). ***The information in iMIT and WebSIS MUST** **match***.
          - **NOTE**: Students ***MUST*** input their U.S. address information in a ***specific way***. Follow the guidance and images on the ISO website [here (https://iso.mit.edu/getting-started/online-check-in/#Tips:~:text=and%20immigration%20systems.-,U.S.%20Postal%20Addressing%20Standards,-Students%20MUST%20input)].

      - **“5. Upload Your I-94 Electronic Record/Card (*AFTER* *ARRIVAL*)”**
        - Provide a copy of a recently issued *I-94 Arrival/Entry Record (https://i94.cbp.dhs.gov/search/recent-search)* – ***NOT*** the “*Travel History*” information.
        - **NOTE**: To obtain the required *I-94 Arrival/Entry Record*, a student ***MUST*** go to the U.S. CBP website and select “Get Most Recent I-94/I-95 (https://i94.cbp.dhs.gov/I94/#/recent-search)”.

      - **“6. Upload Your I-20 or DS-2019 or Visa Category”**
        -
          - Provide a copy of a signed and dated Form I-20.
          - **NOTE**: Students are ***REQUIRED*** to print their MIT-issued Form I-20, sign, and date the document with “wet ink” ***BEFORE*** uploading a copy to iMIT.

      - **“7. Your Immigration Status”**
        -
          - Provide a copy of their completed Immigration Orientation eCourse quiz result showing their ***full name, the date of completion, and a passing score of 90% or higher***.

- **ATTENTION: All documentation must be uploaded in accepted formats**:
  - PDF, JPG, GIF, BMP, and PNG) and are under 2MB in size.
  - Confirm that your uploaded files are not from Dropbox/”The Cloud”.
  - Ensure no special characters or incorrect file names are in the documents (e.g., “doc.pdf.”).
  - All documentation uploaded ***MUST*** be clear and legible.

**STEP 3: Activating U.S. Immigration Status**

- After the above steps have been completed (i.e., Step 1, the Immigration Orientation eCourse/Quiz (https://iso.mit.edu/getting-started/orientation-program/), and Step 2, the Immigration Online Check-In (https://iso.mit.edu/getting-started/online-check-in/)), an ISO staff member will review the student’s information and documentation.
  - This review is typically completed within ***10 business days***, provided that everything submitted is ***complete and accurate***.

- Once the *Immigration Online Check-In* e-Forms are approved, the ISO will send a confirmation email to the student’s @mit.edu address. The confirmation will indicate that:
  - The “Immigration Holds” have been cleared.
  - The student ***MUST*** complete their full-time enrollment/registration for the academic term to formally transition from "Initial" F-1 status to "Active" F-1 status in the U.S.

|   |

**Summary: Required Steps to Activate U.S. Immigration Status**

All **new/incoming international students (https://iso.mit.edu/getting-started/orientation-program/#WhoNewStu) *MUST*** complete the following five (5) conditions to activate their U.S. immigration status and remain in compliance with U.S. immigration regulations:

- 1) Complete the ISO Immigration Orientation eCourse and Quiz.
  - Can be completed from inside or outside the U.S. and requires a passing score of 90% or higher on the quiz.

- 2) Complete the Immigration Online Check-In.
  - This step can ***ONLY*** be completed ***AFTER*** the student has entered the U.S. in F-1 status, passed the orientation eCourse and quiz, and is within 30 days of the program start date listed on the Form I-20 document.
    - **ATTENTION**: ***To avoid delays/rejections of this requirement***, ***carefully follow the detailed instructions and image examples on the ISO Online Check-In webpage (https://iso.mit.edu/getting-started/online-check-in/)***.

- 3) Wait for ISO approval of the Online Check-In e-Form.
  - Once the *Immigration Online Check-In* e-Form has been approved, the “Immigration Holds” will be cleared.

- 4) Register and enroll full-time for the academic term to formally transition from "Initial" F-1 status to "Active" F-1 status in the U.S.
- 5) Submit requests to the ISO or U.S. government agencies ***ONLY*** when the following are true:
  - It is ***AFTER*** the program start date listed on the MIT-issued Form I-20, ***and***
  - The student has been ***physically present*** in the U.S. for at least ***15 business days*** following that date.

**Submitting Immigration Requests**

***Common Requests & Eligibility Criteria***

- **Form I-20 Updates & Travel Signatures**: Can ***ONLY*** be requested (https://iso.mit.edu/visas-and-travel/entering-the-u-s/#ReqTravelSig) after ***ALL*** five (5) conditions above are met.
- **Social Security Letters & SSN Applications**: Can ***ONLY*** submit a “Letter of Social Security (F-1) (https://imit.mit.edu/istart/controllers/client/ClientEngine.cfm?serviceid=EFormLetterofInvitationF1Student0ServiceProvider)” request in iMIT to apply for a Social Security Number (SSN (https://iso.mit.edu/employment/social-security-number-ssn/)) if ***ALL*** five (5) of the conditions above are met ***– AND –*** the student can provide proof of U.S. work/employment and/or funding from MIT.
  - **NOTE**: Students ***NOT eligible*** for an SSN may follow the ISO instructions (https://iso.mit.edu/employment/social-security-number-ssn/#SSNDenialLetter) to request an “SSN Denial Letter” from the Social Security Administration (SSA). For more details, refer to Module 9 of this eCourse.

- **Driver’s License or Massachusetts State ID**: Are ***ONLY*** eligible to apply for or renew a Massachusetts Driver’s License (https://iso.mit.edu/how_to_guide/obtain-a-us-drivers-license/) or a Massachusetts State/Liquor ID (https://www.mass.gov/rmv-issued-identification-cards) if ***ALL*** five (5) of the conditions above are met.
  - **NOTE**: Students driving with a foreign driver’s license ***MUST* *comply*** with all Massachusetts RMV requirements (https://www.mass.gov/service-details/driving-in-massachusetts-on-a-foreign-drivers-license). For more details, refer to Module 9 of this eCourse.

**IMPORTANT REMINDERS**

***Compliance with U.S. Immigration Regulations***

- All new/incoming international students (https://iso.mit.edu/getting-started/orientation-program/#WhoNewStu) are ***REQUIRED*** to have completed the five (5) conditions mentioned above to activate their U.S. immigration status and remain in compliance.

***"Off-Campus" Work/Employment Authorization Restrictions***

- All new/incoming international students (https://iso.mit.edu/getting-started/orientation-program/#WhoNewStu) are ***NOT ELIGIBLE*** for any type of “Off-Campus” work/employment authorization until they meet all the requirements listed in Module 7 of this eCourse.
  - U.S. federal regulations place ***strict limitations*** on work/employment opportunities for international students in the U.S. ***All work/employment MUST be authorized in advance, including but not limited to***:
    - All paid or unpaid opportunities.
    - All on-campus or off-campus opportunities.
    - All work/employment for organizations inside or outside the U.S.
    - **Prior authorization *MUST* be obtained *BEFORE* engaging in any "Off-Campus" work/employment-related activities**. ***NO EXCEPTIONS***.

  - **IMPORTANT**: Engaging in work/employment without prior authorization is a ***serious violation*** of a student’s immigration status. ***Consequences include, but are not limited to***:
    - Termination of a student’s U.S. immigration status.
    - Students’ inability to continue or complete their MIT program.
    - Loss of future U.S. immigration benefits (e.g., CPT, OPT, H-1B, etc.).
