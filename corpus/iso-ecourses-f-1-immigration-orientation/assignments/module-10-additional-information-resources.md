Maintaining academic integrity (https://firstyear.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/OSCCS-AI-1Pager-Electronic-Version.pdf) is a core expectation at MIT. All students are responsible for understanding and upholding these standards throughout their educational journey.

- **OGE’s Academic Integrity Guide**: This guide (https://oge.mit.edu/gpp/roles-relationships/academic-integrity/) offers support on navigating MIT’s academic culture, which may differ from previous educational experiences.

***Avoiding Plagiarism & Academic Misconduct***

- Understand what constitutes plagiarism (https://integrity.mit.edu/handbook/what-plagiarism/) and how to credit sources in all assignments and research work appropriately.
  - If unsure about an assignment, topic, or class policy, seek clarification early by speaking with the instructor or a teaching assistant (TA).

  - Review the course syllabus carefully for details about expectations, grading, collaboration rules, and deadlines.

****When in doubt, ask for help (https://integrity.mit.edu/handbook/helping-you-succeed-resources/)! Being proactive protects academic standing and supports long-term success***.

**“Culture Shock” & Adjustment: What to Expect**

Adapting to life at MIT and in the U.S. can be exciting but also challenging. It’s normal to experience “Culture Shock (https://iso.mit.edu/life-at-mit/cultural-adjustment/cultural-adjustment-phases/)”, especially when adjusting to a new country, language, academic system, and social environment.

***What is “Culture Shock”?***

- “Culture Shock” is a natural response to being in an unfamiliar environment. It can show up as:
  - Homesickness, frustration, or confusion.
  - Feeling isolated or overwhelmed.
  - Withdrawal or even mild depression.
  - These feelings are normal and typically improve over time, often within a few days or weeks.

***The Cultural Adjustment Process***

- While everyone adjusts differently, many go through these common stages:
  - **Honeymoon Phase**: Everything feels new, exciting, and engaging.
  - **Culture Shock**: Challenges and frustrations related to cultural differences begin to surface.
  - **Adjustment/Recovery**: Beginning to understand the new environment and develop coping strategies.
  - **Adaptation/Integration**: Feeling more comfortable, confident, and able to thrive in the new setting.
    - **Tip**: Be patient with oneself and others. Friends, classmates, spouses, or children may be in different phases of adjustment (https://iso.mit.edu/life-at-mit/cultural-adjustment/cultural-adjustment-phases/).

***“Reverse Culture Shock”***

- When returning home, an individual may experience a “Reverse Culture Shock (https://2009-2017.state.gov/m/fsi/tc/c56075.htm)” as they readjust to life in their home country. This, too, is common and part of the transition process.

*****Remember, students are not alone. Students who are struggling should consider speaking with someone (https://medical.mit.edu/services/mental-health-counseling) for support.*****
