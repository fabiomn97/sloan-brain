**Module 5: Maintaining F Immigration Status**

**REQUIRED: A student must confirm that they *"have read and understand all the information within this module”* by marking this module as "DONE" at the bottom of the page.**

As mentioned earlier in this eCourse, while the ISO is here to provide guidance, ultimately, it is the ***student’s responsibility*** to maintain their F-1 immigration status. This requires students’ ***understanding and compliance*** with all U.S. immigration rules and regulations, which, during this time of heightened immigration scrutiny, is more important than ever before. ***Therefore, students MUST review this eCourse carefully and thoroughly***.

|   |

**How Can Students Maintain Their U.S. Immigration Status?**

*****Understanding How To Maintain F Status – Six (6) Key Points*****

**1) Student Responsibilities**

Maintaining a valid U.S. immigration status is a ***critical responsibility*** for all international students at MIT. To stay compliant, students ***MUST*** regularly read ISO emails and newsletters sent to their @mit.edu email account, as these communications contain essential updates related to their U.S immigration status.

- The ISO ***ONLY*** sends messages when the information is important. We do not send unnecessary emails. As the only MIT office authorized to provide immigration-related updates, our messages should be read and acted upon promptly.
- Many ISO communications are automated through iMIT and tailored to each student’s specific circumstances and timelines, including important deadlines and requirements. ***Failing to stay informed about immigration requirements and updates can lead to severe consequences***.
- Immigration rules can change at any time, as seen during the COVID-19 pandemic and with the current U.S. administration. ***These changes can significantly affect international students, so it’s crucial to stay informed, even with a busy academic schedule***.

|   |

**2) Maintaining Full-Time Registration/Enrollment**

All international students are ***required*** to maintain full-time registration/enrollment (https://registrar.mit.edu/registration-academics/student-status/registration-load-light-load) during each mandatory academic term, such as Fall and Spring.

***What Counts as Full-Time Registration/Enrollment?***

Full-time enrollment equals 36 units per term, or the number required by the academic department to be considered full-time.

- At least two subjects (equivalent to 24 units) ***MUST*** be in-person coursework with weekly in-person components.
  - **NOTE**: Only one subject (a minimum of 12 units) can be offered in hybrid or fully online formats per term. ***Anything above this is a violation of U.S. immigration regulations (https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/students/study/full-course-of-study#:~:text=Online%20courses%20and%20%E2%80%9CDistance%20Learning%E2%80%9D)***.
  - For graduate programs with required RA/TA appointments that permit a maximum of 24 units in addition to the RA/TA appointment, at least 12 of those 24 units ***MUST*** include in-person components.
    - **Example**: If the RA/TA role itself involves in-person responsibilities.

***Registration/Enrollment Deadlines***

- **Full-time Registration/Enrollment Requirements**: All international students ***MUST*** complete their full-time registration requirements within ***29 days of “Registration Day”*** for each academic term.
  - **Example**: For Fall 2025, Registration Day is September 3. Therefore, full-time registration ***MUST*** be completed ***BEFORE October 1, 2025***.

- **Registration/Enrollment Exceptions**: Full-time registration is ***not required*** during Institute vacation periods (e.g., IAP and Summer), ***unless*** the academic program requires coursework during that term. (e.g., expected summer graduation)
  - **Example**: If a student is scheduled to graduate in the summer term, full-time registration/enrollment is ***required*** during that period.

- **ATTENTION**: Students unable to register full-time by the required deadline ***MUST*** plan to take a *Leave of Absence (LOA (https://registrar.mit.edu/registration-academics/student-status/leaves-absence-returns))*, with prior approval from the following offices:
  - The International Students Office (ISO (https://iso.mit.edu/about-iso/student-advisors-by-program/)).
  - The student’s academic department.
    - (Undergraduates) Student Support Services (S3 (https://studentlife.mit.edu/wellbeing-support/student-support-services-s3/)).
    - (Graduates) The Office of Graduate Education (OGE (https://oge.mit.edu/gpp/admissions-and-registration/registration-changes/)).

***Dropping Below Full-Time Registration/Enrollment***

**IMPORTANT****:** Dropping below full-time registration/enrollment at any point (***during or after a term***) without prior authorization is a ***violation*** of U.S. immigration regulations (https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/students/study/full-course-of-study).

- **Undergraduate** students ***MUST*** obtain prior approval from:
  - The International Students Office (ISO (https://iso.mit.edu/about-iso/student-advisors-by-program/)).
  - The student’s academic department.
  - Student Support Services (S3 (https://studentlife.mit.edu/wellbeing-support/student-support-services-s3/)).

- **Graduate** students ***MUST*** obtain prior approval from:
  - The International Students Office (ISO (https://iso.mit.edu/about-iso/student-advisors-by-program/)).
  - The student’s academic department.
  - The Office of Graduate Education (OGE (https://oge.mit.edu/gpp/admissions-and-registration/registration-changes/)).

**3) Maintaining F Status & Immigration Documents**

International students ***MUST*** ensure that their immigration documents remain valid ***at all times*** and that the program end date listed on the Form I-20 is ***always*** ***accurate***.

***Form I-20 Program End Date & Extensions***

- If a student anticipates that they will be unable to complete their academic program by the end date listed on their Form I-20, ***it is their responsibility to***:
  - Notify their ISO Advisor (https://iso.mit.edu/about-iso/student-advisors-by-program/) well in advance of the program end date, and
  - Submit a request for a Form I-20 Extension (https://iso.mit.edu/maintaining-status/extending-your-current-program/) through iMIT.
  - **IMPORTANT**: The ISO recommends submitting the extension request ***at least 60 days*** ***before*** the current Form I-20 program end date.
    - Once a Form I-20 has expired, the ISO may not be eligible to issue an extension, which can lead to a loss of the student’s legal immigration status and benefits (such as CPT or OPT).

  - **Reviewing and Keeping Form I-20s**: Always review the details on a Form I-20, especially the “Program of Study” start and end dates.
    - ***NEVER*** discard previously issued Forms I-20. Past documents are ***required*** for future applications (e.g., OPT or STEM OPT).

***U.S. Immigration “Grace Period”***

- Students in F-1 status, along with any F-2 dependents, are allowed to remain in the U.S. for up to ***60 days*** after one of the following:
  - The completion of the academic program, or
  - The end of the OPT or STEM OPT authorization period.
    - This 60-day timeframe is referred to as the “Grace Period (https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/2019/07/students-understand-your-post-completion-grace-period)”, which students and dependents may use to prepare for their departure from the U.S. or to apply for a change in U.S. immigration status.

- **Grace Period Advantages**: During the 60-day “Grace Period”, a student, along with any F-2 dependents, ***MUST*** plan to:
  - Depart the U.S.
  - Transfer to a new U.S. Institution/program.
  - Change to another U.S. immigration status.

- **Grace Period Restrictions**: During the 60-day “Grace Period”, specific rules apply:
  - ***Work and Study*** ***Restrictions***: Absolutely ***NO*** work/employment or formal study is allowed.
  - ***Departure and Reentry*** ***Restrictions***: If an F-1 student leaves the U.S. during the “Grace Period” without first applying for Post-Completion OPT, their F-1 SEVIS record/status (and any F-2 dependent records/statuses) will ***automatically terminate*** upon departure. As a result, reentry to the U.S. in F status would not be possible.
    - **ATTENTION** **– F-1 Students With Dependents**: During the “Grace Period”, it is ***required*** that all F-2 dependents depart the U.S. ***at the same time*** as the F-1 student, ***unless*** the F-1 student has applied for Post-Completion OPT (Post-OPT) ***before*** any departure from the U.S.
      - **Example**: If a student completes their degree on May 29, 2026, has ***NOT*** applied for Post-Completion OPT, and departs the U.S. on June 3, 2026, they ***cannot*** return to the U.S. in F-1 status, and any F-2 dependents would be ***required*** to leave the U.S. with them on June 3, 2026.

**4) Maintaining Student Information**

International students are ***required*** to report any changes to personal or academic information (via iMIT (https://imit.mit.edu/) or WebSIS (http://student.mit.edu/cgi-docs/student.html)) ***within 10 calendar days of the change***.

***Update WebSIS***

- Student ***MUST*** update WebSIS (http://student.mit.edu/cgi-docs/student.html) for changes related to:
  - U.S. address (residential and mailing)
  - U.S. Phone number
  - Email address(s)
  - Legal/biographical information
  - Detailed instructions (https://iso.mit.edu/maintaining-status/) are available on the ISO website.

***Update iMIT***

- Student ***MUST*** update iMIT (https://imit.mit.edu/) for changes related to:
  - Program end date (e.g., extension or early degree completion).
  - Change of major or academic program.
  - Change in degree level (e.g., bachelor’s to master’s).
  - Changes in funding sources.
  - Early departure, withdrawal, or leave of absence.
  - ***NOTE****: This list highlights common updates but is not exhaustive. Students should consult the ISO website (https://iso.mit.edu/) if unsure where to report a change.*

Massachusetts state law (https://www.mass.gov/info-details/health-care-reform-for-individuals) and MIT (https://health.mit.edu/my-mit/internationals/understanding-insurance) ***require*** that all students, along with any dependents, maintain valid health insurance coverage while in the U.S.

- **NOTE**: The MIT Student Health Insurance Plan (MIT SHIP (https://mit.myahpcare.com/)) meets Massachusetts state health insurance requirements.

***MIT Student Health Insurance Plan (MIT SHIP)***

- Students and MIT’s Student Health Insurance Plan (MIT SHIP (https://mit.myahpcare.com/)):
  - Students who are registered full-time or enrolled in at least 27 units per term are automatically enrolled in and billed for MIT SHIP.

- For all questions about MIT health plans or general health insurance questions, review their website (https://mit.myahpcare.com/) or contact MIT Health (https://health.mit.edu/) directly.

**6) Maintaining U.S. Immigration Status & Taxes**

**IMPORTANT****: ISO staff and advisors are *NOT* trained in U.S. tax law and *CANNOT* provide tax advice. For assistance, refer to official resources and the ISO *Taxes* webpage (https://iso.mit.edu/maintaining-status/taxes/).**

***Who Must File***

All individuals in F-1 or F-2 status are ***REQUIRED*** to file at least one U.S. federal tax form ***each calendar year***, even if no income was earned.

***Filing Deadline***

- The annual deadline to submit tax forms is April 15 of every calendar year.
  - **Example**: Anyone ***physically present*** in the U.S. ***at any point*** from January 1 to December 31, 2025, ***MUST*** ***file taxes by April 15, 2026***.

- This requirement applies every year an individual is in F-1 or F-2 status and continues until they have been outside the U.S. or out of F status for a full year.

***Required Identification***

To file taxes, individuals ***MUST*** have either:

- A Social Security Number (SSN (https://iso.mit.edu/employment/social-security-number-ssn/)), or
- An Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN (https://www.irs.gov/individuals/general-itin-information)).
  - **NOTE**: Students who need an ITIN may apply for one when filing their tax return, using the ISO-provided tax software, “Sprintax”.

***Resources and Support***

- The ISO provides access to tax preparation software and workshops beginning in late February/early March each year. For full details, visit the ISO *Taxes* webpage (https://iso.mit.edu/maintaining-status/taxes/).

**IMPORTANT: Once again, a****ll individuals in F-1 and F-2 status are *REQUIRED*** **to file at least one U.S. tax form every year, *regardless of income*. To avoid missing the deadline and ensure compliance with this immigration requirement, it is strongly recommended to set a calendar reminder *NOW*.**
