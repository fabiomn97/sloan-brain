**Module 6: Work/Employment Information For F-1 Students**

**REQUIRED: A student must confirm that they *"have read and understand all the information within this module”* by marking this module as "DONE" at the bottom of the page.**

***Work/Employment Authorization Is Always Required — NO EXCEPTIONS***

U.S. federal regulations place ***strict limitations*** on work/employment opportunities for international students in the U.S. ***All work/employment MUST be authorized in advance, including but not limited to***:

- All paid or unpaid opportunities.
- All on-campus or off-campus opportunities.
- All work/employment for organizations inside or outside the U.S.
  - Students ***MUST*** obtain proper authorization ***BEFORE*** beginning any work/employment opportunities.

**IMPORTANT**: Engaging in work/employment without prior authorization is a ***serious violation*** of a student’s immigration status. ***Consequences include, but are not limited to***:

- Termination of a student’s U.S. immigration status.
- Students’ inability to continue or complete their MIT program.
- Loss of future U.S. immigration benefits (e.g., CPT, OPT, H-1B, etc.).

*****Remember, always consult with an ISO Advisor (https://iso.mit.edu/about-iso/student-advisors-by-program/) BEFORE accepting or beginning any work/employment opportunities while in F-1 status*****

**On-Campus Work/Employment**

***What Is Considered “On-Campus” Work/Employment?***

F-1 students are eligible to engage in “On-Campus (https://iso.mit.edu/employment/on-campus-employment/f-1-on-campus-work-information/)” work/employment activities without obtaining prior authorization from the ISO or USCIS, but ***ONLY*** after the program start date listed in the “Program of Study” section on page 1 of their Form I-20. **NOTE: Compliance with U.S. federal regulations (https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/students/work/working-in-the-united-states#:~:text=the%20United%20States%3A-,On%2DCampus,-Off%2DCampus) regarding “On-Campus” work/employment is *CRUCIAL* for all F-1 students**.

- **“On-Campus” work/employment is defined as *ANY* paid or unpaid activities that meet one or more of the following criteri**a:
  - Performed at MIT.
  - For MIT.
  - Funded by MIT.

***IMPORTANT******: What Is NOT Considered On-Campus Work?***

All work/employment activities connected with entities/organizations ***other than MIT***, regardless of form, payment, or location (i.e., paid or unpaid, remote or in-person, inside or outside the U.S.), is considered “Off-Campus (https://iso.mit.edu/employment/)” work/employment, and students ***MUST*** obtain prior authorization from the ISO and/or USCIS ***BEFORE*** participating in these activities.

- **Examples of “Off-Campus” work/employment include, but are not limited to**:
  - Activities with a professor’s private company, even if based at MIT.
  - Consulting or freelance work/employment.
  - Entrepreneurial activities or self-employment (https://iso.mit.edu/student-content-access/#:~:text=Post%2DGraduation%20Work%20Visas%20%26%20Permanent%20Residence%20Options%20%E2%80%93%20February%208%2C%202024).
  - Participation in paid or unpaid internships.
  - Involvement in startups, whether student- or faculty-led.
  - Publishing or presenting at conferences (https://iso.mit.edu/employment/publications-conferences-and-employment/).
  - Participation in experiential learning or non-MIT fellowships with service components.
  - Activities for a non-U.S. company while physically located in the U.S.
  - Activities for a U.S. company, whether located inside or outside the U.S.
  - Activities affiliated with another school, university, or institution.

***“Off-Campus” Work/Employment Eligibility Restrictions: Read Carefully***

- **New/Incoming Students**
  - All new/incoming international students (https://iso.mit.edu/getting-started/orientation-program/#WhoNewStu) are ***NOT*** eligible to apply for any “Off-Campus” work/employment (whether paid or unpaid) until completing two full academic terms (e.g., fall and spring) in F-1 status at MIT and while physically present in the U.S.
    - **NOTE****:** In addition to the two-term requirement, undergraduate students ***MUST*** officially declare a major, approved by both the academic department and the Registrar, ***BEFORE*** becoming eligible to apply for “Off-Campus” work/employment authorization.

  - **ATTENTION**: **International students who begin their MIT program in Fall 2026 will become eligible to apply for “Off-Campus” work/employment authorization *no earlier than Summer 2027*.**

**REMINDER: If the work/employment activities are *NOT* for MIT, then prior “Off-Campus” work/employment authorization is *REQUIRED*.**

**"On-Campus" Work/Employment Restrictions**

***Hours-Per-Week Restrictions***

- The total number of hours for ***ALL*** “On-Campus” work/employment ***combined*** is subject to ***strict limits***:
  - During MIT academic terms (e.g., fall and spring), all "On-Campus" work/employment is ***limited to 20 hours per week or LESS***.
  - During official MIT vacation periods (e.g., summer or winter/IAP), all "On-Campus" work/employment can be ***more than 20 hours per week***, but ***ONLY*** if coursework/enrollment is ***NOT*** required by the student’s academic program or department during that time.

***Special Circumstances for RAs, TAs, and Fellowships***

- Research Assistantships (RA), Teaching Assistantships (TA), and Fellowship Awards ***with service components*** are equivalent to the ***maximum*** ***20 hours per week*** "On-Campus" work/employment limit for F-1 students.
- **NOTE**: Some fellowships may not be categorized as "On-Campus" work/employment if they lack service components.

**Volunteering**

***Definition of Volunteering***

The U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) defines “Volunteering (https://iso.mit.edu/employment/volunteering/)” as a "True Volunteer Position (https://webapps.dol.gov/elaws/whd/flsa/docs/volunteers.asp)" where no compensation or benefits are provided or expected in return for services or activities performed. These roles are typically with recognized civic, charitable, or humanitarian organizations, such as:

- Homeless shelters
- Hospital volunteer programs
- Schools
- Religious institutions (e.g., churches, synagogues, mosques, or temples)

**IMPORTANT REMINDER**: If an activity involves any form of payment, benefits, or services, or ***DOES NOT*** clearly meet the DOL’s definition of volunteering, it is considered “Off-Campus (https://iso.mit.edu/employment/)” work/employment. As such, these activities would ***REQUIRE*** prior authorization from the ISO and/or USCIS ***BEFORE*** participation can occur.

**Entrepreneurship & Start-Ups**

***Can F-1 Students Pursue Startups and/or Entrepreneurial Activities?***

This is a common question among international students. The ISO has consulted with representatives from SEVP and DHS to provide clear guidance.

- ***In general:***
  - F-1 students may be permitted, as long as it ***DOES NOT*** go beyond drafting a business plan and/or is part of early-stage entrepreneurial exploration.
    - Any active involvement beyond planning, such as fundraising, manufacturing, incorporation, recruiting partners, or hiring employees, is considered “Off-Campus” work/employment and ***REQUIRES*** prior authorization from the ISO and/or USCIS.

  - ***IMPORTANT******:***
    - These activities ***DO NOT*** qualify as volunteer work, even if they are unpaid.
    - For full details, refer to the ISO website (https://iso.mit.edu/employment/), which provides comprehensive information regarding work/employment (https://iso.mit.edu/employment/#CommonQs) and entrepreneurship (https://iso.mit.edu/student-content-access/#:~:text=Post%2DGraduation%20Work%20Visas%20%26%20Permanent%20Residence%20Options%20%E2%80%93%20February%208%2C%202024).

**Important Reminders About U.S. Work/Employment**

- ***Prior Authorization Is ALWAYS Required***
  - U.S. immigration regulations define work/employment based on the nature of the activities performed in the U.S., ***regardless of payment***.
  - Unauthorized work/employment (even if unpaid) is a ***VIOLATION*** of one's immigration status and can have ***serious consequences***.

- ***On-Campus Work Hour Limits MUST Be Followed***
  - Students are ***NOT*** permitted to exceed the allowed number of “On-Campus” work/employment hours during a required academic term (20 hours per week or ***LESS***).
  - Exceeding this limit is also considered a ***VIOLATION*** of one's immigration status and can have ***serious consequences***.

**Students are *responsible* for maintaining compliance with all work/employment-related regulations. When in doubt, consult the** **ISO** (https://iso.mit.edu/about-iso/student-advisors-by-program/) ***BEFORE* engaging in any non-MIT-related work/employment activities.**
