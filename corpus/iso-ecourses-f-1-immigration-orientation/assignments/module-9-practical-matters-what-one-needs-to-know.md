**F-2 Dependents: Spouse & Children**

***Key Requirements for F-2 Status (https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/students/get-started/bringing-dependents-to-the-united-states)***

- **Work/Employment:** Individuals in F-2 status are ***NOT allowed*** to engage in any form of work/employment, including but not limited to:
  - All paid or unpaid opportunities.
  - All on-campus or off-campus opportunities.
  - All work/employment for organizations inside or outside the U.S.

- **Full-Time Academic Study** :
  - **F-2 Spouse**: Full-time academic study (https://www.ice.gov/doclib/sevis/pdf/Nonimmigrant%20Class%20Who%20Can%20Study.pdf) is ***NOT allowed***. Part-time enrollment is permitted in certificate or vocational programs.
  - **F-2 Children**: Full-time study is **ONLY** permitted at the kindergarten through 12th grade (K–12) levels.

Additional details are available on the ISO “Life at MIT” webpage (https://iso.mit.edu/life-at-mit/) under the section titled *Spouses, Parents, and Families (https://iso.mit.edu/life-at-mit/spouses-parents-and-families/)*.

**Social Security Number (SSN)**

***What is an SSN?***

- A Social Security Number (SSN (https://iso.mit.edu/employment/social-security-number-ssn/)) is a unique identifier issued by the U.S. Social Security Administration (SSA (https://www.ssa.gov/)) for tax reporting and benefit tracking purposes. ***SSNs DO NOT***:
  - Authorize work/employment.
  - Serve as a national ID.
  - Prove U.S. citizenship or permanent residency.

- **IMPORTANT*****:*** Once issued, an SSN remains a student’s ***lifelong identifier***, and students should:
  - Memorize the number and keep it in a safe place.
  - Remember that it can be used during future visits to the U.S.
  - ***DO NOT lose or discard it**.*

- **NOTE**: An SSN is ***NOT*** ***required*** to obtain a driver's license, open a bank account, sign a lease, or apply for a loan. However, individuals receiving income in the U.S. ***MUST*** have an SSN for tax reporting and payment purposes.

***Who Is NOT Eligible for an SSN?***

The following individuals do not qualify for an SSN:

- **F-1 Students *without U.*S. Work/Employment**: May request an “SSN Denial Letter (https://iso.mit.edu/employment/social-security-number-ssn/#SSNDenialLetter)” for purposes such as applying for a Massachusetts driver’s license (https://iso.mit.edu/how_to_guide/obtain-a-us-drivers-license/) or state ID (https://iso.mit.edu/how_to_guide/obtain-a-state-id/). Receiving this letter ***DOES NOT*** prevent future SSN eligibility.
- **F-2 Dependents (Spouse or Children)**: Ineligible for work/employment and therefore ***NOT*** eligible for an SSN. An “SSN Denial Letter (https://iso.mit.edu/employment/social-security-number-ssn/#SSNDenialLetter)” may be requested if needed for state-based services.

***Who MUST Apply for an SSN?***

An SSN is ***REQUIRED*** for anyone engaged in U.S. work/employment, including:

- On-campus positions.
- Research Assistantships (RA).
- Teaching Assistantships (TA).
- Other paid roles.

***SSN Eligibility Requirements***

To be eligible for an SSN, students ***MUST*** meet the following criteria:

- Be registered/enrolled as a full-time student at MIT.
- The registration/enrollment has been sent from the ISO to SEVIS (new/incoming students (https://iso.mit.edu/getting-started/orientation-program/#WhoNewStu)).
- Be physically present in the U.S. for ***at least 15 business days*** ***AFTER*** the start date listed on the MIT “Certificate of Eligibility” immigration document (Form I-20 or Form DS-2019).
- The work/employment start date falls within 30 days of the SSN application date.
- ***Have All Required Documentation (https://iso.mit.edu/employment/social-security-number-ssn/#SSNInfoF1)**:
  - Proof of RA/TA/Fellowship or on-campus work/employment (https://iso.mit.edu/employment/social-security-number-ssn/#:~:text=I%2D20(s).-,Proof%20of%20U.S.%20Work/Employment,-%3A%20Acceptable%20documentation%20includes).
  - Proof of off-campus work/employment (https://iso.mit.edu/employment/social-security-number-ssn/#:~:text=A-,letter%20from%20an%20off%2Dcampus%20employer,-(example%20here)%2C%20on%20letterhead%20and%20signed%20that).
  - An SSN Eligibility Letter (https://iso.mit.edu/employment/social-security-number-ssn/#:~:text=If%20employed%20by%20MIT%2C%20the%20student%20must%20obtain%20a%20letter,F%2D1)%E2%80%9D%20eForm.%20NOTE%3A%20Not%20required%20for%20Alumni%20students.) issued by ISO.

***SSN Application Process***

**How to Apply**:

- Complete the online SSN application (https://www.ssa.gov/number-card) through the SSA website.
- Visit a local SSA office (https://secure.ssa.gov/ICON/main.jsp) in person within 45 calendar days of submitting the online application.
- *****Bring all required documents (https://iso.mit.edu/employment/social-security-number-ssn/#SSNInfoF1).
- The SSN card will be mailed within approximately 14 business days after the in-person submission is accepted.
- Detailed instructions are available on the ISO *Social Security Number (SSN)* webpage (https://iso.mit.edu/employment/social-security-number-ssn/).

***Additional Requirements***

- **Form I-9**: All individuals beginning work/employment ***MUST*** complete the *Form I-9 Employment Eligibility Verification (https://www.uscis.gov/i-9)* within three (3) business days of starting any work/employment. An SSN is ***NOT required*** to complete the Form I-9.
  - For information on MIT employment, refer to the *I-9 Process at MIT* webpage (https://hr.mit.edu/i-9) on the MIT Human Resources (MIT HR) website (https://web.mit.edu/).

**U.S. Forms of Identification & Driving in Massachusetts**

***Massachusetts Driver’s License and State ID***

International students are eligible to apply for a Massachusetts Driver’s License (https://iso.mit.edu/how_to_guide/obtain-a-us-drivers-license/) or a Massachusetts State/Liquor ID (https://www.mass.gov/rmv-issued-identification-cards), depending on individual needs:

- A Driver’s License (https://www.mass.gov/passenger-class-d-drivers-licenses) allows legal operation of a motor vehicle in the U.S.
- A State/Liquor ID (https://iso.mit.edu/how_to_guide/obtain-a-state-id/) serves as a government-issued form of identification for individuals who do not plan to drive.
- **NOTE**: As of July 2023, under the *Work and Family Mobility Act (https://www.mass.gov/news/registry-of-motor-vehicles-announces-standard-drivers-licenses-regardless-of-immigration-status-available-soon-under-work-and-family-mobility-act)*, eligible Massachusetts residents may apply for a Standard (Class D or M) Driver’s License regardless of immigration status.

***Application Process & Information***

- For detailed information, please refer to the Massachusetts Registry of Motor Vehicles (RMV) website. To visit the RMV in person, students are required to schedule an appointment online through the ‘myRMV Online Service Center’ webpage.
- In-person appointments are ***REQUIRED*** to visit the Massachusetts Registry of Motor Vehicles (RMV (https://www.mass.gov/topics/driving-motor-vehicles)) and must be scheduled through the *myRMV Online Service Center (https://atlas-myrmv.massdot.state.ma.us/myrmv/_/)*.
- **Applicants *MUST* Provide All Required Documentation**:
  - Social Security Number (SSN) card or “SSN Denial Letter (https://iso.mit.edu/employment/social-security-number-ssn/#SSNDenialLetter)”.
  - Valid passport.
  - Printed and signed Form I-20.
  - Proof of current U.S. address (e.g., lease/rental agreement, utility bill, etc.).
  - Current driver’s license (if applicable).
  - Payment for the application fee.
  - **NOTE**: Additional documents may be required depending on individual circumstances. Full details are available on the Massachusetts RMV website (https://www.mass.gov/topics/driving-motor-vehicles).

***Important Reminders***

- **Timing**: It is recommended to wait ***at least 15 business days*** after the program start date listed on the Form I-20 before submitting or renewing a driver’s license or a state ID application.
- **Insurance**: When renting a vehicle, personal liability and accident insurance are ***strongly advised***.
- **Foreign Driver’s Licenses**: Students who drive using a foreign driver’s license ***MUST* *comply*** with all requirements outlined by the Massachusetts RMV [here (https://www.mass.gov/service-details/driving-in-massachusetts-on-a-foreign-drivers-license)].

**Stay Protected Against Scams** ***[IMPORTANT]***

***Awareness Is Key***

- Scams (http://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/scam) targeting international students are unfortunately common across the U.S., including in the MIT community. These schemes often involve attempts to steal money or personal information through deception and intimidation. Recognizing common warning signs can help prevent financial loss and identity theft.

***Examples of Common Scams***

- **Fake Tax Refunds**: Third-party services falsely claiming to file tax returns on behalf of students to collect refunds.
- **Rental Scams**: Requests for deposits on housing where no actual rental exists or keys are never provided.
- **Online Purchase Fraud**: Scams on platforms such as Craigslist, eBay, or PayPal involving fake listings or non-delivery of items.
- **Imposter Calls**: Individuals posing as immigration officials demanding payment for fabricated "visa fees" or "international student taxes"—often via gift cards or wire transfers.
- **Green Card Lottery Fees**: Websites charging money to enter the official Diversity Visa (Green Card) Lottery, which is free through the U.S. government.

***Common Scam Tactics***

- Claims of money owed, legal violations, or accusations of fraud.
- Use of threats, fear, or intimidation to force immediate action.
- Requests that require immediate action/urgent responses, or payment without verification.
- Threats of arrest, deportation, or visa cancellation if no action is taken.
- Instructions by the scammers are not to hang up or contact anyone else during the call.
- Use of official/legal-sounding language such as “federal law,” “visa compliance,” or “immigration enforcement” to appear legitimate.

***What to Know About Scams [IMPORTANT]***

***Report Suspicious Activity Immediately***

- Contact MIT Police (https://police.mit.edu/) and the ISO (https://iso.mit.edu/about-iso/contact-us/) ***immediately*** to report any of the following:
  - Suspicious or threatening phone calls, emails, or letters.
  - Communications demanding immediate action or payment.
  - Unethical behavior by employers requesting payment for job offers or agencies offering to create false credentials.
  - Requests for money or personal information from unknown individuals, especially those involving threats of arrest or deportation.

***Key Guidance & Facts About Scams***

- Legitimate U.S. government agencies (https://www.usa.gov/agency-index) will ***NEVER*** request or demand immediate payment by phone or demand gift cards, wire transfers, or checks.
- U.S. government communication typically comes by mail or from an official government email account, ***NEVER*** through unsolicited phone calls or unverified emails.
- U.S. government contacts will allow time to verify information or call back later. Immediate demands for a response or payment are ***always scams***.
  - **NOTE**: The Department of Homeland Security (DHS (https://www.dhs.gov/)) may contact students regarding their SEVIS record, but will ***NEVER*** request money or sensitive personal information.

***How to Stay Protected***

- ***ALWAYS*** request the caller’s full name, badge number, and phone number, and inform them that the MIT Police (https://police.mit.edu/) or an ISO Advisor (https://iso.mit.edu/about-iso/student-advisors-by-program/) will return the call. ***DO NOT*** feel pressured to stay on the phone.
- ***NEVER*** share or disclose personal information, such as your Social Security Number (SSN), bank account details, passport information, or immigration status, over the phone or via email with unknown contacts.
- ***AVOID*** cashing unexpected checks or signing contracts without reading and fully understanding them.
- ***BE CAUTIOUS*** of unsolicited calls from scholarship services, phone companies, or individuals claiming to be “officials.”
- ***AVOID*** answering questions with a simple “yes,” which could be recorded and misused.

***Why Reporting Matters***

- When one person reports a scam, ISO can help protect the entire international student community. Sharing this information strengthens campus safety for everyone.

***Additional Scam Resources***

- The U.S. Federal Trade Commission (FTC) has information pamphlets (https://www.consumer.ftc.gov/features/feature-0033-avoiding-scams-information-recent-refugees-and-immigrants) available in several languages.
- USCIS webpages on common scams (https://www.uscis.gov/avoid-scams/common-scams) and how to avoid (https://www.uscis.gov/avoid-scams) and report scams (https://www.uscis.gov/scams-fraud-and-misconduct/avoid-scams/report-immigration-scams).
- The IRS website (https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/tax-scams-consumer-alerts) has information on how to report tax scams (https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/tax-scams-how-to-report-them) and common tax scams (https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/dirty-dozen).
- For more information on staying safe, please visit the ISO *Campus Safety* webpage (https://iso.mit.edu/life-at-mit/campus-safety/#:~:text=Emergency%20Information-,Scams%20and%20Fraud,-MIT%20Police).
