**Module 4: U.S. F-1 Student Immigration Status & Documents**

**REQUIRED: A student must confirm that they *"have read and understand all the information within this module”* by marking this module as "DONE" at the bottom of the page.**

**What is F-1 Student Status?**

F-1 status is a non-immigrant student category that allows international students to study full-time in the U.S. Students in F-1 status must follow specific federal regulations (https://iso.mit.edu/maintaining-status/) to maintain their legal U.S. immigration status.

- **NOTE: Students not *physically inside* the United States have no U.S. immigration status.**

|  **F-1 Student Immigration Status Reminders** |

|

**Source of Funding**

 |

F-1 students must maintain verified proof of financial support that was issued within the past six (6) months. Acceptable funding sources include:

- Personal or family support.
- Scholarships, assistantships, or fellowships.
- Any combination of the above

 |

|

**Dependents**
***(Spouses & Children***
***of F-1 Student)***

 |

F-2 dependents are ***NOT*** permitted to have work/employment (paid, unpaid, or for a company outside the U.S.) if they are physically inside the U.S.

F-2 dependents ***CANNOT*** engage in full-time study*. They may take part-time, recreational courses, such as language classes.

***NOTE**: F-2 children may enroll in full-time study only at the elementary or secondary school level (i.e., K-12).

 |

|

**Grace Period**

 |

Individuals in F-1 status (and their dependents) have a 60-Day Grace Period (https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/2019/07/students-understand-your-post-completion-grace-period) to leave the U.S. after completing their:

- Degree program, or
- Period of Practical Training (Post-OPT or STEM OPT).

**IMPORTANT**: The grace period ***DOES NOT*** apply if a student withdraws, takes a leave of absence (LOA), or fails to complete their degree program.

 |

|

**Health Insurance Requirements**

 |

U.S. immigration regulations do not require F-1 students or F-2 dependents to have U.S. health insurance. However, the Commonwealth of Massachusetts (https://www.mass.gov/info-details/health-care-reform-for-individuals) and MIT require all F-1 students to have health insurance coverage. Refer to MIT Medical’s website (https://health.mit.edu/my-mit/students) for full details.

 |

***Understanding a U.S. Immigration Status***

- The term “U.S. Immigration Status” (sometimes called “U.S. Visa Status”) refers to an individual’s legal permission to be inside/remain in the U.S.
- An individual is ***ONLY*** considered to be in a U.S. immigration status while ***physically*** in the U.S.
- A visa sticker/stamp in a passport is used for entry to the U.S. and ***DOES NOT*** determine an individual’s U.S. immigration status while inside the country.
- ***ONLY*** three (3) documents determine an individual’s immigration status inside the U.S., and their valid F status:
  - **1) Unexpired Passport**: Remember, a passport can ***NEVER*** expire; it ***MUST*** be valid for six (6) months into the future, ***at all times***.
  - **2) Valid Form I-20**: ***MUST*** reflect accurate information ***at all times***, have a valid “Program End Date”, and should include a valid travel signature if planning international travel.
  - **3) Valid I-94 Arrival/Departure Record**: Confirms the individual's official admission to the U.S. in the correct U.S. immigration status (F-1 or F-2). ***MUST*** be checked and saved upon every entry into the U.S.

**Important Immigration Documents**

**1. Passport**

***Passport Validity Requirements**:*

- **Passports *MUST* remain valid *at all times*** with a minimum of six (6) months’ validity remaining before expiration.
- Students should initiate the renewal process at least eight (8) months ***before*** the document's expiration, either through their home country’s embassy/consulate (https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/consularnotification/ConsularNotificationandAccess.html) in the U.S. or abroad.

***Expired Passport with Valid Visa:***

- If the F-1 entry visa is still valid in an expired passport, it is still usable for travel.
- In this case, students would be required to carry both the expired passport (with the valid visa) and the new valid passport when entering the U.S.

***Lost or Stolen Passport:***

- If a passport is lost or stolen, take the following steps:
  - File a police report in the city/location where the passport was last seen.
  - Contact one’s embassy/consulate (https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/consularnotification/ConsularNotificationandAccess.html) to request a replacement passport.
  - If the lost passport is later found, notify both the police and the embassy/consulate with this update.

***2. Form I-94 Arrival/Departure Record***

***What is an I-94 Arrival/Departure Record?***

- The I-94 Arrival/Departure Record (https://iso.mit.edu/visas-and-travel/entering-the-u-s/obtaining-the-form-i-94/) is the official U.S. government record of a non-U.S. citizen’s entry into the U.S. through a U.S. Point-of-Entry (POE (https://www.cbp.gov/about/contact/ports)). For F-1 students, the I-94 Record confirms their immigration status and is ***crucial*** for maintaining legal status while in the U.S.

***How to Access the I-94 Record:***

- 1) Visit the U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) website (https://i94.cbp.dhs.gov/home#:~:text=for%20I%2D94-,Get%20Most%20Recent%20I%2D94/I%2D95,-View%20Travel%20History).
  - **Tip**: Use Chrome browser for best results.

- 2) Select the "***Get Most Recent I-94***" tab.
  - ***DO NOT*** select “*Apply for New I-94*” or “*View Travel History*” tabs.

- 3) Enter personal details exactly as they appear in the passport and/or F-1 visa sticker/stamp:
  - First Name
  - Last Name
  - Date of Birth (Month–Day–Year)
  - Passport Issuing Country
  - Passport Number

***Save and Verify the I-94 Record After Every U.S. Entry:***

- **IMPORTANT****: A copy of the I-94 Record (https://i94.cbp.dhs.gov/search/recent-search) *MUST* be downloaded, reviewed, and saved after *EVERY* entry into the United States, as the I-94 number changes with each entry.**
  - **NOTE**: It is the student's ***responsibility*** to verify all the I-94 Record information on the document and retain a copy for their U.S. immigration records and/or for future immigration purposes. ***DO NOT FORGET!***

***What to Check on the I-94 Record:***

- After accessing the record, confirm the following:
  - The “**Class of Admission**” section ***MUST*** state “*F-1*” (or “*F-2*” for dependents).
  - The “**Admit Until Date**” section ***MUST*** state “*D/S*” (for “*Duration of Status*”).

***Trouble Accessing or Correction Required for an I-94 Record?***

**IMPORTANT**: If any information on the I-94 Record is incorrect or missing, follow the steps listed on the ISO website [here (https://iso.mit.edu/visas-and-travel/entering-the-u-s/obtaining-the-form-i-94/#I94Issues)].

- Allow 5–15 business days for CBP to update the record following the submission of a correction request (https://iso.mit.edu/visas-and-travel/entering-the-u-s/obtaining-the-form-i-94/#I94Issues).
- If not resolved, students can contact CBP (https://www.cbp.gov/about/contact/ports/deferred-inspection-sites#:~:text=Baltimore-,Boston,-Buffalo) at:
  - **Location**: Logan International Airport – Terminal E, Lower Level, Room 500, Boston, MA 02128
  - **Hours**: 8:00 am - 2:00 pm, Monday to Thursday
  - **Phone**: (617) 568-1810 (Option 1)
  - **Email**: boston.i94@cbp.dhs.gov

- For issues related to Form I-515A (https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/students/travel/form-i-515a-overview) issued at the port of entry, students can contact their ISO Advisor (https://iso.mit.edu/about-iso/student-advisors-by-program/) for assistance.

***3. Form I-20 (Certificate of Eligibility) Document***

***Key Information About the Form I-20:***

The Form I-20 is a vital immigration document for all individuals in F-1 or F-2 status. The following responsibilities help ensure the student’s compliance with U.S. immigration regulations:

- **MEMORIZE** **the SEVIS ID Number**
  - This unique number (found at the top of the I-20) is essential for visa applications and immigration processes.

- **MONITOR** **the Program End Date**
  - Set a calendar reminder ***NOW*** to track the "Program End Date" listed on the Form I-20, ***as it CANNOT expire***. This will help avoid unintended status violations.

- **ENSURE** **the I-20 Remains Valid**
  - ***DO NOT*** allow a Form I-20 to expire. Extension requests (https://iso.mit.edu/maintaining-status/extending-your-current-program/) ***MUST*** be submitted well in advance of the document’s expiration.

- **REVIEW** **for Accuracy and Updates**
  - Always confirm that all the information on the Form I-20(s) is correct. Request corrections promptly if needed.

- **CHECK** **for a Valid Travel Signature**
  - ***BEFORE*** booking and/or traveling outside the U.S., students ***MUST*** always check both the travel signature date (listed on page 2) and the “Program End Date” (listed on page 1) on their Form I-20.
  - **IMPORTANT**: **A travel signature *MUST* be valid when entering the U.S. (https://iso.mit.edu/visas-and-travel/entering-the-u-s/entering-the-u-s-continuing-f-1-students/) from abroad.**
    - A travel signature ***remains valid*** for multiple trips, for **12 months** from the travel signature date (page 2) ***– OR –*** until the “Program End Date” (page 1) — ***whichever date comes first***.
      - **NOTE**: After a student completes their degree program at MIT and begins a period of Practical Training (Post-OPT or STEM OPT (https://iso.mit.edu/visas-and-travel/traveling-during-f1-opt-stem-opt/)), a travel signature ***remains valid*** for multiple trips, but ***only*** for a period of 6 months from the date of the travel signature.

- **KEEP** **All Form I-20s Issued**
  - ***Never*** discard any Form I-20s, including those previously used or marked as “replaced.”
  - This includes all Form I-20s issued for both F-1 students and F-2 dependents.

***4. Entry Visa Sticker/Stamp***

***Key Information About the F Visa Sticker/Stamp:***

An F visa sticker/stamp is placed inside a passport and ***MUST*** be valid for entry into the U.S.

- **ATTENTION**:
  - This document is ***REQUIRED*** for all international students, except for citizens of Canada and Bermuda (https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/tourism-visit/citizens-of-canada-and-bermuda.html) who travel to the U.S. with their country-eligible passport.
  - This document is ***ALLOWED*** to expire while an individual is inside the U.S.
  - This document ***CANNOT*** be issued from within the U.S.
  - Always review the visa sticker/stamp ***BEFORE*** booking any travel.

- **What to Check Before Traveling:**
  - **Visa Expiration Date**: Memorize the expiration date and remember that a visa sticker/stamp is allowed to expire while a student is inside the U.S.
    - **IMPORTANT**: If the visa sticker/stamp has expired and the individual travels outside the U.S., a new visa sticker/stamp ***MUST*** be obtained from a U.S. consulate/embassy abroad ***BEFORE*** the individual is eligible to return to the U.S. in F status.

  - **Number of Entries Allowed**: Check the "***Entries***" section on the visa sticker/stamp. This explains how many times the visa sticker/stamp can be used to enter the U.S.
    - **“M” = Multiple Entries**: The document allows for multiple entries into and exits from the U.S. as needed, provided the visa sticker/stamp, passport, Form I-20, and I-94 Record are valid.
    - **“1” or “S” = Single Entry**: The document allows for only one (1) entry into the U.S. If an individual exits the U.S., even for a short trip, a new visa sticker/stamp is ***required*** for re-entry.
    - **“2” = Two Entries**: The document allows for only two (2) entries into the U.S. and exits from the U.S. After the second entry, a new visa sticker/stamp must be obtained for any future return to the U.S.

- **Visa Type/Class**: The visa sticker/stamp should list "F-1" for students in F-1 status and “F-2” for dependents.
- **Annotation Section**: This section shows the SEVIS ID number, school name, and location.
  - If the annotation includes a previous SEVIS ID number and/or school information (such as in the case of a transfer or return from absence), this is normal and not a cause for concern.

***Important Visa Sticker/Stamp Reminders***:

- **A Helpful Analogy**: Think of the F visa sticker/stamp like a key to a house: A key is needed to unlock the door and enter a house, but once inside, the key is not required to stay or move around the house. The key is only ***required*** again if exiting and then re-entering the house.
- A visa sticker/stamp ***cannot*** be issued from within the U.S.
- A valid F visa sticker/stamp is ***required*** to enter the U.S., except for citizens of Canada and Bermuda (https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/tourism-visit/citizens-of-canada-and-bermuda.html).
  - After entering the U.S., a visa sticker/stamp can expire while the individual remains legally in the U.S.
  - A new F visa sticker/stamp is only ***required*** if the individual leaves the U.S. and needs to return in F status.

- As long as their other immigration documents (passport, I-94, and Form I-20) are valid, the individual may:
  - Stay in the U.S.
  - Transfer to another U.S. school, institution, or program.
  - Apply for U.S. work/employment authorization (e.g., CPT (https://iso.mit.edu/employment/f-1-curricular-practical-training/) or OPT (https://iso.mit.edu/employment/#:~:text=F%2D1%20Pre,STEM%20OPT%20Extension)).
