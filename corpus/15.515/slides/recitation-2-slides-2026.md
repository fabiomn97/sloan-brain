Revenue Recognition Intuition

- Earnings = Revenues – Expenses

- Intuition: record revenue when it is
- Earned
- Reasonably collectible

- Expenses matched to revenues by matching principle

## Page 5

Revenue Recognition Principles

- Revenue Recognition Principles:

1. Contract with customer (What is enforceable?) There must be a legally enforceable contract (written, oral or implied) with clear commercial substance (i.e. a signed software agreement or a purchase order)

2. Performance obligations (Service/product defined?) You must identify each distinct good or service promised in the contract (i.e. a phone sold with a one-year service plan counts as two obligations: the handset and the service)

3. Transaction price (Price determined?) There must be a price determined

4. Allocate price (Bundled service or upfront payment?) If there are multiple performance obligations, allocate the transaction price to each one based on their stand-alone selling prices

5. Recognize revenue (as performance obligations met) Finally, recognize revenue when you satisfy each performance obligation, either at a point in time (i.e. delivery of goods) or over time (i.e. subscription)

Allowance for Doubtful Accounts Review

- Allowance for Doubtful Accounts (ADA), or AR Reserve, is a contra asset account associated with Accounts Receivable (A/R).

- (Gross A/R – ADA) = Net A/R

- When ADA is used, the company is anticipating that some accounts will be uncollectible in advance of knowing the specific account or amount.

- As a result, the bad debt expense is often an estimate in order to make revenue to more closely match the sale  CECL (Current Expected Credit Loss model)

## Page 29

Example: A/R turnover & days receivable

Understanding Days Receivables – a super simple example

Imagine you sell one pack of gum per day for US $1. Nobody in your class carries cash, so every sale is on one-day credit (they pay the next day). Daily sale: 1 gum = US $1 Year-end (Dec 31) total: 365 gums = US $365 in revenue On December 31, you sell one more gum, but the buyer will pay on January 1. That means you finish the year with Accounts Receivable (AR) of US $1.
- AR = US$ 1
- Annual Revenue = US$ 365
- A/R Turnover = 365 times
- Days receivable (365 / turnover) = 1 day Your Days Receivable is 1 day, meaning you collect receivables, on average, the very next day—exactly how your gum sales work.

Days Receivable shows how long, on average, it takes to collect cash from customers.

Summary

-  Understand why firms allow purchase on credit

- Companies can increase sales by making purchases easier for

customers
- But, this could have downward impact on earnings (bad debt,

returns)
- Understand the role of estimates as per CECL
- Reasonably estimate bad debt by maintaining a contra-asset

account (ADA)
- Recognize those estimates as they are matched with revenues
- Key equations
- AR (beg) + Credit Sales – Collections – Write-offs = AR (end)
- ADA (beg) + BDE – Write-offs = ADA (end)
- AR, net = AR, gross – ADA
