## Slide 6 — BASE equation for changes in balance sheet amounts

- Every balance sheet account uses the same BASE equation. For example, for inventory:
- Beginning balance (inventory at start of year)
- + Additions (e.g., purchases)
- - Subtractions (e.g. cost of goods sold)
- = Ending balance

## Slide 7 — The Inventory Equation (Modified)

- Assume inventory accounts are updated at the end of fiscal period, as opposed to after every sale/ purchase
- Inventory Equation:
- Beginning inventory + Additions – Cost of goods sold = Ending Inventory
- Beginning Inventory + Additions = Cost of goods sold + Ending Inventory
- 7
- This is known from prior period
- This is tracked within a period
- Cost Flow Assumptions allocate the sum of Beg. Inv. and Additions between COGS & End Inv.
- Total Costs Available

**Speaker notes:** In a perpetual inventory system, inventory and cost of goods sold accounts are updated with each sale. Perpetual inventory system is usually used for expensive items, such as jewelry, cars, etc. Cost flow assumptions are only necessary for Periodic inventory system. Loss of inventory can be in COGS if it is trivial – this is a cost of doing business If the Loss is material (e.g., fire destruction), it will be recognized as a separate line item.

## Slide 15 — Adjusting LIFO to FIFO – The LIFO Reserve

- Different “cost layers” of Inventory at the end of Year 2

- If companies use LIFO, they are required to disclose the current cost of their LIFO inventory in their footnotes
- The difference between current cost and LIFO inventory value is referred to as the LIFO Reserve
- Most companies will disclose the following:
- LIFO Reserve = Ending InvFIFO – Ending InvLIFO

## Slide 20 — Management discussion and Analysis disclosure

- MD&A Disclosure:
- In 2021, Kroger had lower operating profits (by $197 million) because they used LIFO
- In 2020, Kroger had higher operating profits (by $7 million) because they used LIFO
- Most firms tell you the effect of LIFO on net income (what would income be under FIFO), but this disclosure is not required.  Firms are required to disclose the effect of LIFO on ending inventory, and you can use this to derive the effect on income.  See slides after the conclusion for the math.

## Slide 21 — Why?

- Why do we have LIFO and why do we need to know how to adjust from LIFO to FIFO?
- LIFO exists because of tax reasons
  - Firms with rising costs want to report taxable income using LIFO  because reported profits are lower
- LIFO Conformity rule:
  - If a firm uses LIFO for tax purposes, it must also use LIFO for financial reporting purposes
  - This is a rare instance where financial reporting must be same as tax reporting

## Slide 23 — Financial Statement Analysis Issues: Ratios Involving Inventory

- Overview: calculate key metrics for Kroger in 2021 after adjusting LIFO to FIFO
- Inventory Turnover = COGS / Average Inventory
- Measure of how fast the firm is able to sell its inventory. Higher turnover, faster sales of inventory
- Days inventory = 365 / Inventory Turnover
- Measure of the average period (in days) that the good is held as inventory before being sold
