## Slide 20 — NHL

    - As part of the CBA negotiation, players and owners had to agree on when revenues and costs are included in a team’s income.
    - Why do accounting/timing issues like these matter? Isn’t cash the same over the life of the deal?

## Slide 21 — NHL – rules for discussion

    - We will now split the class in half
    - Half of you take the role of Emilie Castonguay (negotiator representing the players), and the other half take the role of Gary Bettman (representing the owners)
    - Focus your analysis on the representative transactions for one team in the NHL
    - You should articulate whether revenues/expenses should be recognized in 2019, and if so, how much
    - This will ultimately determine the profitability of that team, and the terms of the revenue sharing agreement over the next 5 years

## Slide 30 — conclusion

- What does the NHL example tell us?
- Firms engage in transactions where goods and services are provided during year T, and cash is either received in year T-1 or year T+1 – this can have a dramatic effect on performance measures
- Determining how to account for these transactions has the potential to affect multiple stakeholders
- There are accounting rules and there is “accounting judgment”. Lots of accounting decisions involve estimates, and discretion in how to apply the rules
