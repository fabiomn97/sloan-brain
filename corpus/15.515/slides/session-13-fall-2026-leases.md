## Slide 8 — Changing accounting standards

- Rules for leasing have changed. Effective date for fiscal year ends after December 15, 2018 (2021 for private firms)
  - Under old rules, companies could structure leases to keep them off the balance sheet
  - For example, Kroger could structure leases such that there would be no asset or liability on B/S.
    - Why would they want to do this?
- Two types of leases under new GAAP rules:
  - Financing leases (Sometimes called “ownership leases”)
  - Operating leases (Sometimes called “rental leases”)

## Slide 10 — Accounting Criteria For Finance Leases

- Intuition: Does the lessee control the asset?
- ASC 842: A lease is a finance lease if it meets ANY of these criteria (Example of a “bright-line rule”):
- The lease transfers ownership to the lessee by the end of the lease term
- The lease term is most of the economic life of the underlying asset
- The present value of the payments equals or exceeds the fair value of the underlying asset
- Bargain purchase option (e.g., can buy asset for $1 at end of lease)
- There is no alternative use to the lessor at the end of the lease term

## Slide 11 — Finance Lease: an Example

- Kroger leases 30 buildings. Assume:
- The buildings have a current cost of $300,000
- The expected life of the buildings is 30 years
- The lease term is 30 years
- Kroger’s borrowing rate is 6%
- The annual lease payment is $21,795 per year
- Is this a Finance Lease? (Yes)
- Lease term is 100% of the asset’s useful life
- Present value of lease payments is 100% of fair value

## Slide 12 — Finance Lease: An Example

- What is the finance lease obligation?
- Present value of 30 lease payments of $21,795
- PV of $1 annuity, 30 years, 6% = 13.765
- Present value of the lease payments is $21,795K * 13.765 ≈ $300,000

## Slide 21 — Quick note about “rental leases”

- 21
- Operating leases: Quite similar to financing leases but
  - Economically, more closely mimics renting than owning
  - Requires more advanced accounting
- Under new rules, now on balance sheet as asset and liability
- 1
- 2
- Key Distinction: Equal expense each year under operating (“rental”) leases
  - Creates smoother and more predictable impact on earnings
  - Total payment is negative to CFO Lower CFO under operating leases
    - Under finance lease: Interest is CFO, but principal payments are CFF
