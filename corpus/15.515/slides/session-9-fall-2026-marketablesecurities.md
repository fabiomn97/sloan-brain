## Slide 7 — Statement of Financial Accounting Standard (SFAS) 115 (now modified by ASU 2016-01)

- SFAS 115 requires firms to classify securities into 3 categories based on the intent of the acquirer:
- 1  Held-to-maturity (debt only)
  - Acquired with ability and intent to hold to maturity
    - Ability and intent? Cash flow needs, past behavior, market conditions, etc.
- 2  Trading securities (debt and equity)
  - Acquired for short-term profit potential
- 3  Available for sale (debt only)
  - Securities not classified as either of above
  - Before 2016: equity could be classified as available for sale. Now, debt only
- 7

## Slide 8 — Statement of Financial Accounting Standard (SFAS) 115

- Accounting treatment of unrealized gains/losses varies across categories:
- B/S		     I/S
- Effect		   Effect
- Held-to-maturity (debt only)			(Q1) no
- Available for sale (debt only)		 	(Q1) yes 	 (Q2) no
- Trading securities (debt & equity)	(Q1) yes 	 (Q2) yes
- Changes in market value affect the balance sheet for available for sale AND trading securities
- Changes in market value affect the income statement ONLY for trading securities
- 8

## Slide 9 — What happens to gains & losses on afs securities?

- “AOCI” = Accumulated Other Comprehensive Income:
- A stockholders’ equity account that is not reflective of net income
- Records gains/losses on Available For Sale (“AFS”) securities (among others. E.g., pension items, currency translation, hedging)
- The unrealized gains and losses for a particular period that are added (or subtracted) to AOCI are often referred to as “OCI”

## Slide 20 — Reclassifications of Marketable Securities (FAS 115)

- This will not be on your Exam:
- Reclassification from Trading to Available for Sale
- Gains/losses of the period recognized on reclassification date
- Subsequent market value changes reported in “Accumulated Other Comprehensive Income”
- Reclassification from Available for Sale to Trading
- Cumulative gains or losses, including those of current period, recognized on reclassification date
- Subsequent market value changes reported in the Income Statement

## Slide 22 — FAIR VALUE terminology

- “Level 1”
- Values based on quoted prices in active markets for identical assets/liabilities
- “Level 2”
- Values based observable inputs other than Level 1
- (e.g., quoted prices for similar assets/liabilities, or interest rates, or yield curves)
- “Level 3”
- Values based inputs observable only to the reporting entity
- (e.g., management estimates or assumptions)
- 22
- Example:  A stock or bond of a company traded on an active exchange.
- Example:  A bond that is infrequently traded, but similar to actively traded bonds.
- Example:  An exotic, illiquid security; a privately held firm, an operating asset that is judged to be impaired.

## Slide 24 — Earnings management

- Potential due to the difference in reporting the changes in fair market value
- To report higher income (shift OCI gains to net income)
  - Management can reclassify available-for-sale investments as trading investments
    - Allows unrealized gains to be reported on the income statement
  - Management can sell available-for-sale investments to recognize a gain (or loss) and buy back later
