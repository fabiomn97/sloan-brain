- “Companies record goodwill on their books when they buy a business for more than the value of its hard assets, such as property and equipment.
- The buyer must test the fair value of its reporting units each year and, if that figure is less than the amount on the balance sheet, impair the goodwill.
- The $7.3 billion goodwill impairment Kraft Heinz Co. announced ... is the largest such write-down in the U.S. consumer staples industry in at least a decade.”
- “This goodwill impairment alone is greater than that entire sector over the last three years,” said Carla Nunes, a managing director at Duff & Phelps.

## Slide 18 — Goodwill Impairment

- Kraft Heinz writes off $7.3 billion of goodwill.
  - How does this affect Kraft Heinz financial statements?
    - Assets decline and a loss is recognized (usually below operating income)
  - How do market participants interpret goodwill impairments?
    - Suggests market value of segment has fallen below book value
    - Bad acquisitions limit support for future initiatives
    - Calls for CEO to be fired – give up all compensation

## Slide 25 — conclusion

- 1.  In the U.S. and under IFRS all firms are required to use purchase accounting
- 2.  How to allocate purchase price?
  - Tangible Assets recorded to reflect fair (=market) value
  - Identifiable intangibles added to balance sheet and amortized
  - Goodwill as a plug for unaccounted for purchase price
- 3.  Accounting for goodwill after the acquisition is very controversial, has the potential to have large impact on financial statements but in a very untimely manner.
