## Slide 7 — What happens if we make a “mistake” in our estimate of losses? (I of II)

- We must adjust the Allowance for Doubtful Accounts balance, so the net accounts receivable accurately reflect what we expect to collect on our accounts receivable.
- But this also means that we must also either recognize additional expenses in future periods or increase future income to recognize errors in accounting for bad debt expenses.
- As a result, inaccuracies in current period estimates affect future income (we do not restate old balances).

## Slide 30 — The Inventory Equation (Modified)

- Assume inventory accounts are updated at the end of fiscal period, as opposed to after every sale/ purchase
- Inventory Equation:
- Beginning inventory + Additions – Cost of goods sold = Ending Inventory
- Beginning Inventory + Additions = Cost of goods sold + Ending Inventory
- 30
- This is known from prior period
- This is tracked within a period
- Cost Flow Assumptions allocate the sum of Beg. Inv. and Additions between COGS & End Inv.
- Total Costs Available

**Speaker notes:** In a perpetual inventory system, inventory and cost of goods sold accounts are updated with each sale. Perpetual inventory system is usually used for expensive items, such as jewelry, cars, etc. Cost flow assumptions are only necessary for Periodic inventory system. Loss of inventory can be in COGS if it is trivial – this is a cost of doing business If the Loss is material (e.g., fire destruction), it will be recognized as a separate line item.

## Slide 57 — Why?

- Why do we have LIFO and why do we need to know how to adjust from LIFO to FIFO?
- LIFO exists because of tax reasons
  - Firms with rising costs want to report taxable income using LIFO  because reported profits are lower
- LIFO Conformity rule:
  - If a firm uses LIFO for tax purposes, it must also use LIFO for financial reporting purposes
  - This is a rare instance where financial reporting must be same as tax reporting
