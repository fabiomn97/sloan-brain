Financial Accounting (15.515)

Self-Assessment Problem Set 3

Instructions: The following ten multiple choice questions are designed to provide you with an opportunity to assess how well you understand the material we have covered to date. You do not have to submit a solution to these questions, they will not be graded, and you can use AI if it helps you ensure you understand the material. There will be at least one correct answer to each multiple-choice question. On the exam, you would need to get all of them correct to receive credit for the question.

[A] IFRS Research and Development A firm reporting under IFRS incurs research and development expenditures during the year. Which statement(s) is/are correct? 1. Research expenditures are generally expensed as incurred. 2. Development expenditures that meet the capitalization criteria may be recorded as an asset. 3. Capitalizing qualifying development costs increases current-period pretax income relative to expensing those same costs immediately. 4. Capitalized development costs can never reduce future-period income. 5. None of the above.

[B] Capitalized Development Costs A firm capitalizes qualifying development expenditures and later records amortization on the resulting asset. Which statement(s) is/are correct? 1. The initial capitalization increases total assets relative to immediate expensing. 2. Amortization reduces the carrying amount of the development-cost asset. 3. Amortization reduces current-period pretax income. 4. Over the entire life of the project, capitalization necessarily creates more total pretax income than immediate expensing. 5. None of the above.

[C] Depreciation Estimates A company uses straight-line depreciation. At the beginning of the current year, it increases an asset’s estimated remaining useful life and reduces its estimated residual value. Which statement(s) is/are necessarily correct? 1. The company must restate depreciation expense reported in prior years. 2. The change is treated prospectively. 3. The effect on current-period depreciation depends on the magnitude of the two estimate changes. 4. The asset’s original historical cost changes. 5. None of the above.

[D] Sale of Property, Plant, and Equipment A company sells equipment for cash. The selling price exceeds the equipment’s net book value but is less than its original cost. Which statement(s) is/are correct? 1. The company recognizes a gain. 2. Cash increases. 3. Gross PP&E decreases. 4. Accumulated depreciation related to the equipment is removed from the accounts. 5. None of the above.

[E] Useful Lives and Earnings Two otherwise identical airlines purchase identical aircraft for the same price on the same date and use straight-line depreciation. Airline X selects a shorter useful life than Airline Y, with the same residual value. During the early years of the aircraft’s life, which statement(s) is/are correct? 1. Airline X will report higher depreciation expense. 2. Airline X will report lower pretax income.

3. Airline X will report lower net PP&E. 4. Airline X’s cash flows from operations will necessarily be lower because of the higher depreciation expense. 5. None of the above.

[F] Acquisition Accounting and Goodwill A company acquires another business for cash. Which statement(s) is/are correct at the acquisition date? 1. Identifiable assets acquired are generally recorded at fair value. 2. Liabilities assumed reduce identifiable net assets acquired. 3. Goodwill equals the purchase price less the fair value of identifiable net assets acquired. 4. Goodwill is recognized only if the acquired company previously reported goodwill on its own balance sheet. 5. None of the above.

[G] Acquired Intangible Assets A company recognizes a customer-relationship intangible asset in a business combination even though the acquired company had not previously reported that asset. Which statement(s) best explain this treatment? 1. Acquisition accounting can recognize separately identifiable intangible assets at fair value. 2. Internally generated expenditures that created the customer relationships may previously have been expensed. 3. Recognition in the acquisition means the asset must have appeared on the acquiree’s pre-acquisition balance sheet. 4. Recognizing more identifiable finite-lived intangible assets generally reduces goodwill, holding the purchase price constant. 5. None of the above.

[H] Purchase Price Allocation A company acquires another business for cash. Holding the purchase price and liabilities assumed constant, the company assigns $2 billion more of the purchase price to a finite-lived technology intangible asset rather than goodwill. Which statement(s) is/are correct? 1. The technology intangible asset increases by $2 billion. 2. Goodwill decreases by $2 billion. 3. Total assets at the acquisition date are unchanged. 4. Future amortization expense will generally increase. 5. None of the above.

[I] LIFO versus FIFO A firm uses LIFO in a period of generally rising inventory costs. Compared with FIFO, which statement(s) is/are generally correct? 1. LIFO reports higher cost of goods sold. 2. LIFO reports lower ending inventory. 3. LIFO reports lower gross profit. 4. LIFO necessarily reports lower cash from customers. 5. None of the above.

[J] LIFO Reserve A company reports inventory under LIFO and discloses that FIFO inventory would exceed reported inventory by $3.4 billion. Which statement(s) is/are correct? 1. The disclosed amount is commonly referred to as the LIFO reserve. 2. Adding the disclosed amount to reported LIFO inventory provides an approximation of inventory under FIFO. 3. If the reserve increases during the year, FIFO cost of goods sold is generally lower than LIFO cost of goods sold, ignoring taxes and other complications. 4. The reserve represents cash set aside by the company to purchase replacement inventory. 5. None of the above.
