Question 4. Mergers & Acquisitions (15 points total; 5 points each)

On December 31, 2012 Sutherland Corporation has (i) PPE with a book value of $200,000; (ii) Inventory and A/R with a combined book value of $10,000; and (iii) liabilities with a book value of $70,000.  Shroff Company makes an offer to purchase Sutherland for $300,000 in cash, assuming all of the assets and liabilities of Sutherland Corp and Sutherland accepts. When allocating the purchase price, Shroff determines that the market value of the liabilities is $62,000, the market value of PPE is $115,000, and there is an internally developed patent that is worth $17,500 and has a useful life of 20 years. There is no difference between the market value and book value of the working capital (i.e., inventory and A/R).

A. What transaction does Shroff record on the acquisition date? Cash Inventory & AR PP&E Patent     Goodwill Liabilities -$300k +$10k +$115k $17.5k     +219.5k $62k

B. On December 31, 2014 Shroff evaluates the goodwill for impairment and determines it is

worth $45,000. What transaction does he record? Goodwill R/E -$174.5k -$174.5 (goodwill impairment)

C.  For this question ignore parts B of this question.  On December 31, 2012, at the

time he is making the purchase price allocation, Shroff hired two appraisers to independently value the company.  One appraiser valued the patent intangible asset to be worth $35,000, and the other valued the patent intangible asset at $10,000. Which valuation will result in larger income in 2013? Provide two sentences supporting your answer. Income will be larger if the $10k valuation is used. This is because the patent is amortized over 20 years, and the $25k difference goes to goodwill (which isn’t amortized). So net income difference would be ($35k-$10k)/20 years = $1,250/year
