Financial Accounting (15.515)

Solution — Self-Assessment Problem Set 1

Correct choices are shown in bold. Because each question may have more than one correct answer, all correct statements are identified.

A. Unearned Revenue

Correct: 1, 3, and 4

On January 1, cash increases by $7,200 and unearned revenue (a liability) increases by $7,200. Thus, total assets increase, but shareholders’ equity does not.

By January 31, one-sixth of the service has been provided: $7,200 ÷ 6 = $1,200. The company recognizes $1,200 of revenue and reduces unearned revenue by $1,200. Revenue increases shareholders’ equity by $1,200.

B. Accounts Receivable

Correct: 2, 3, and 4

The company has earned $4,600 of revenue but has not yet collected cash. Accounts receivable therefore increases by $4,600.

Recognizing the $4,600 of revenue increases net income and shareholders’ equity by $4,600.

C. Collection of Receivables

Correct: 2 and 3

Collecting an existing receivable increases cash by $3,000 and decreases accounts receivable by $3,000.

One asset is exchanged for another, so total assets do not change. Revenue was recognized when the sale or service occurred, not when the receivable is collected.

D. Prepaid Expenses

Correct: 1 and 2

Cash decreases by $900 and a prepaid maintenance asset increases by $900.

Because the maintenance service will be provided in May, no expense is recognized on April 20. Total assets are unchanged.

E. Accrued Expenses

Correct: 1, 3, and 4

The employees have earned $1,100 that has not yet been paid, so the company records salary expense and a salary payable liability.

The expense reduces net income and shareholders’ equity. Cash does not decrease until the employees are paid.

F. Inventory and Cost of Goods Sold

Correct: 1, 2, and 3

The inventory cost is 400 × $12 = $4,800. A 100% markup over cost means the selling price is twice cost, so sales revenue is $9,600.

Because the sale is on account, accounts receivable increases by $9,600. Inventory decreases by its cost, $4,800—not by the $9,600 selling price.

G. Accounts Payable

Correct: 1, 2, and 3

Purchasing $3,600 of inventory on account increases inventory and accounts payable by $3,600.

Paying $3,000 decreases cash and accounts payable by $3,000. Across both transactions, inventory is up $3,600, cash is down $3,000, and accounts payable is up a net $600.

Neither transaction affects shareholders’ equity.

H. Prepaid Insurance

Correct: 1, 2, and 4

Six months of a $7,200 annual policy have expired by June 30, so insurance expense is $7,200 × 6/12 = $3,600.

The adjustment decreases prepaid insurance by $3,600 and increases insurance expense by $3,600. The expense reduces shareholders’ equity. No cash is paid on June 30 because the policy was paid for on December 31, 2021.

I. Interest Accrual

Correct: 1, 2, and 4

Six months of interest is $9,000 × 8% × 6/12 = $360.

Interest expense and interest payable each increase by $360. The expense reduces shareholders’ equity by $360. Cash is unaffected until the interest is paid.

J. Loan Principal

Correct: 1, 2, and 4

Paying $3,000 of principal decreases cash by $3,000 and decreases the loan payable by $3,000.

Total liabilities therefore decrease by $3,000. Repayment of principal is not an expense and does not affect interest expense.
