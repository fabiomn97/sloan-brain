Part II - Analyzing UFP Technologies 2019 Form 10-K

Amounts in Part II are in thousands of dollars unless otherwise stated.

1. Sales growth, receivables growth, turnover, and days receivable

| Measure | 2019 | 2018 |

| Net Sales | $198,381 | $190,455 |

| Gross A/R | $29,134 | $28,885 |

| Net Sales Growth | 4.2% | - |

| Gross A/R Growth | 0.9% | - |

| A/R Turnover | 6.81x | 6.59x |

| Days Gross A/R | 54 days | 55 days |

Net Sales Growth = (198,381 - 190,455) / 190,455 = 4.2%. Gross A/R Growth = (29,134 - 28,885) / 28,885 = 0.9%. A/R Turnover = Net Sales / ending Gross A/R. Days Receivable = 365 / A/R Turnover.

2. 2019 sales performance

Net sales increased 4.2% from $190.5 million in 2018 to $198.4 million in 2019. Reported sales are earned revenue recognized during the year; deferred revenue represents customer consideration for which revenue has not yet been earned. Ending deferred revenue increased slightly from $2.507 million to $2.574 million.

3. A/R collection performance

Gross A/R increased only 0.9% while sales increased 4.2%. A/R turnover increased from 6.59x to 6.81x and days receivable declined from about 55 days to 54 days. These measures indicate slightly faster collection in 2019.

4. Meaning of the $52 bad-debt reversal

UFP reduced its prior estimate of uncollectible receivables. The reversal decreases the Allowance for Doubtful Accounts, increases net Accounts Receivable, and increases income by $52 thousand.

5. BSE entries for the bad-debt reversal and write-offs

$52 bad-debt reversal:

| Assets | = | Liabilities | + | Stockholders Equity | Income Statement Caption |

| Allowance for Doubtful Accounts |  |  |  | Retained Earnings | Bad Debt Expense 52 reversal |

| 52 |  |  |  | 52 |  |

Because the allowance is a contra-asset, decreasing it by $52 increases net assets by $52. The reversal increases income and equity by $52.

$26 write-offs, net of recoveries:

| Assets | = | Liabilities | + | Stockholders Equity | Income Statement Caption |

| Accounts Receivable     Allowance for Doubtful Accounts |  |  |  |  |  |

| (26)                         26 |  |  |  |  |  |

Gross Accounts Receivable and the related contra-asset both decrease. Net Accounts Receivable and total assets are unchanged, and no new bad-debt expense is recognized at write-off.

6. Did collection quality weaken materially?

Based only on these data, no. Gross A/R grew 0.9% versus 4.2% growth in sales, while days receivable declined from about 55 to 54 days. These measures do not indicate a material deterioration in collection quality.

7. Effect of the bad-debt reversal on the collection analysis

The reversal does not directly affect gross A/R turnover or days gross receivable because both measures use gross A/R. The reversal does indicate that management became less pessimistic about expected credit losses, which is consistent with the collection measures but does not by itself establish collection quality.

| Cash |  | Unearned Revenue |  |  |  |

| 3,216 |  | 3,216 |  |  |  |

The assignment specifies that A/R is not part of these transactions, so the customer consideration increases Cash and Unearned Revenue.

$3,149 of revenue recognized from deferred revenue:

| Assets | = | Liabilities | + | Stockholders Equity | Income Statement Caption |

|  |  | Unearned Revenue |  | Retained Earnings | Revenue 3,149 |

|  |  | (3,149) |  | 3,149 |  |

As the related performance obligations are satisfied, the liability decreases and earned revenue increases equity.

10. Why the $1.7 million from beginning deferred revenue is less than $3.149 million

The $3.149 million includes revenue recognized during 2019 from both the deferred-revenue balance present at the start of the year and amounts newly deferred during 2019. UFP received $3.216 million of additional customer consideration during 2019, and some of those amounts were earned before year-end. Thus only about $1.7 million of the $3.149 million came from the January 1 balance.
