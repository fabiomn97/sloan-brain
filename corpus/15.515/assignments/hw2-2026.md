Background: UFP’s Revenue Recognition Policy

UFP Technologies designs and manufactures solutions primarily for medical devices and other engineered applications. In 2019, substantially all product revenue was recognized when products shipped. Tooling and machinery revenue was recognized primarily upon customer acceptance. Certain customer-owned tooling revenue was recognized over an estimated two-year useful life. Engineering-service revenue was recognized as the services were performed. When customer billing preceded revenue recognition, UFP recorded deferred revenue; when revenue recognition preceded billing, it recorded an unbilled receivable (a contract asset). Source: UFP Technologies, Inc., 2019 Form 10-K, Note 1, Revenue Recognition.

Billing, cash, and other facts

- January 2: UFP invoices Harbor $40,000 for the tooling and engineering services.  “Invoices” means that UFP sent Harbor a bill for the tooling and engineering that has not actually been performed.  From both firms’ perspectives, neither obligation is satisfied.
- January 10: Harbor pays the $40,000 advance invoice in full.
- January 15: UFP ships all 1,200 inserts, and the performance obligation for the inserts is satisfied on the shipment date.  This is known as FOB shipping point by the accounting profession.  UFP invoices Harbor $60,000, and records $36,000 of cost of goods sold.
- February 1: The custom tooling is formally delivered by UFP engineers and accepted by Harbor. For custom tooling, UFP records costs through their service and engineering operations. You can ignore these costs on this date and for the entirety of this assignment.
- February 7, 14, 21, and 28: UFP completes one engineering session on each date.
- February 14: Harbor pays the $60,000 invoice for the inserts.

Required

2. Prepare Harbor’s journal entries using the balance sheet equation for the same dates. Harbor records the inserts as Inventory when received and recognizes no expense during this period. Record accepted tooling as Equipment and engineering sessions as Engineering Expense.

3. Immediately after February 28, what balances related to this contract remain in UFP’s Accounts Receivable and Deferred Revenue accounts? Briefly explain.
