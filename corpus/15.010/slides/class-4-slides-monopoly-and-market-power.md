## Page 31

The Entrant’s Problem: How Do You Start a Network?

Network effects are a barrier to entry: the incumbent’s product is valuable because everyone is already on it. A new product starts with no users.

Direct externality

Two-sided market

Nobody joins a messaging app or a social network with no one on it.

Riders will not come without drivers; drivers will not come without riders.

The chicken-and-egg problem

Who would be the first user of a new messaging app?

So how do you get the first users?

15.010 ·Class 4 ·31

## Page 32

How to Start a Network: Direct Externalities

Answer: go after a small, local, strong network first. Make the product valuable to one tight group that are likely early adopters.

Facebook Harvard only in Feb 2004, then Columbia, Stanford and Yale, then all colleges, then everyone (2006)

Tinder Launched at USC: a party where the ticket was downloading the app, then sorority-by-sorority pitches

Venmo Two Penn roommates paying each other back; spread friend to friend within their circle

Slack One team at a time: the whole team switches together, then it spreads inside the company

WhatsApp Grew through phone contact lists, so it saturated whole countries

Strava Started with about 20 friends who were serious Bay Area cyclists, and the riding buddies they already trained with

Sources: Facebook, Tinder, Venmo, Slack, WhatsApp, Strava 15.010 ·Class 4 ·32

## Page 34

How to Start a Network: Indirect Externalities

Answer: pick one side and subsidize it until the other side has a reason to show up.

Uber New cities: guaranteed hourly earnings for drivers and free first rides for riders, then cut the subsidies

Airbnb Founders went door to door recruiting New York hosts, then sent free professional photographers (2010)

Microsoft Xbox Sold the console below cost; the games and their makers followed the installed base

Credit cards Cardholders pay nothing per transaction and earn rewards; merchants pay the interchange fee

OpenTable Charged restaurants (a monthly fee plus $1 a seated diner) for a system useful on its own; diners always booked free

TaskRabbit Launched in one Boston neighbourhood, Charlestown, in 2008 with about 100 hand-vetted runners, before opening to clients

Sources: Uber, Airbnb, Xbox, Credit cards, OpenTable, TaskRabbit 15.010 ·Class 4 ·34

## Page 61

Today’s Lessons

Market power needs a barrier to entry: something that stops others from eating the margins

Network effects and learning curves are two such barriers, and they cut both ways: explosive growth or unraveling

Sources of market power evolve: Google added them one by one; AI labs are still early

Don’t just profit from it. Manage it and innovate.
