## Page 18

Key result of a single-price monopolist

The key insight from our study of monopoly is that when the monopolist can only set one price, marginal revenue is less than the price

This is the case because in order for the monopolist to sell one more unit she must lower the price, not just on the additional unit to be sold, but on all of the units

## Page 19

This result in pictures

Suppose the inverse demand curve is P = 100 - Q and marginal cost is 10

In this case Revenue = (100 – Q)Q So, Revenue = 100Q – Q2

To maximize profits, the monopolist sets marginal revenue equal to marginal cost

Marginal Revenue = 100 – 2Q

MR = MC

100 – 2Q = 10 Q = 45 => P = 55

## Page 33

A few observations

The monopolist doesn’t like deadweight loss

To the monopolist, the ideal scenario is she finds a way to convert that DWL into PS!!

While she is at it, she would like to convert the CS into PS as well

The DWL gets created because of the nature of the monopolist’s marginal revenue curve

If, and I repeat IF, the monopolist could sell additional units by lowering the price just on the new sales, she would

If she’s really good at this, DWL would be minimal if not zero
