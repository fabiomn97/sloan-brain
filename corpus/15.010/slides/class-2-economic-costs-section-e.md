## Page 59

6.  Economies of Scope

Economies of scope:  producing the next model alongside the current ones costs less than producing it alone.

In developing the model The research carries over The science, the recipes and the people are paid for once and used again.

In serving the model The channel already exists ChatGPT and the API have their users. A new model ships through the same products, with no new distribution to build.

The stack is already built Data pipelines, training code, the safety and evaluation stack: built once, reused.

Distillation The large model trains the smaller, cheaper ones — building one directly lowers the cost of the other.

The test in any industry: find the shared input.

Opportunity Cost and Sunk Cost

## Page 62

Opportunity Cost

Opportunity cost:  the value of the best alternative you give up when you make a decision.

- Using a resource one way is always a decision not to use it another way.

- It never appears in the accounts — no cash changes hands, so nobody records it.

- Economic cost = what you pay out  +  what you give up.

If a resource has an alternative use, using it is never free.

## Page 63

Opportunity Cost: Three Examples

Your own time

Land on game night

Bitcoin miners

An MBA's tuition is the small number. The salary you do not earn for two years is the large one.

A shop near Fenway opens as usual on game night. The real cost of that is not its rent — it's the parking revenue the same ground could have earned instead, many times its Tuesday rate.

Power, land and cooling built for mining can serve AI instead. The cost of mining a bitcoin is the AI earnings given up — what AI would bring in, after what it would cost to serve.

The Wall Street Journal, Dec. 24, 2025

The same resource has a different opportunity cost at a different time, and in a different use.

## Page 64

Sunk Cost

Sunk cost:  a cost already incurred that you cannot recover, whatever you decide next.

- It is identical under every option on the table, so it cannot change which option is best.

- The question is never “what have we already spent?” but “what happens from here?”

- Spent is not the same as wasted — but either way, it is gone.

Sunk costs are irrelevant to the decision in front of you.

The Sunk-Cost Fallacy

The fallacy:  carrying on because of what you have already spent, rather than what you will get.

Concorde Britain and France went on funding a supersonic airliner after it was clear there was no economic case for it. The fallacy is often named after it.

SoftBank and WeWork Billions in, then billions more — “a classic rookie error,” said the Journal.

Sunk Means Unrecoverable

Lessons:  Opportunity and Sunk Cost

1 The true cost of running your own cluster is what it could earn instead — and opportunity costs never appear on the statement.

2 A rental market turns the GPUs from a fixed cost into a variable one.

3 Sunk means unrecoverable: an open-weights run is sunk, a cluster with a rental market is not.

## Page 78

Today's Lessons

1 Fixed or variable is about the contract and the horizon, not the input.

Buying turns a variable cost into a fixed one, and moves it to the balance sheet.

2 Average cost falls with scale, and with scope.

Scale: spreading fixed costs, learning, bargaining power. Scope: one shared input serving several products.

3 Count the opportunity cost. Ignore the sunk cost.

The one that matters most is never in the accounts. The one that is there, and shouldn't count, is already gone — so walk out of more movie theaters.
