Assume that there are four firms operating in a competitive wholesale electricity market. Their generating assets are listed below:

Firm Plant Fuel Type Capacity (MW) Variable Cost

a) Assume the market is perfectly competitive and all firms are price takers. Explain how one could construct the supply curve from this data. What would be the supply at prices $20 and

$40, respectively?


Order all plants from lowest to highest variable cost and add capacity cumulatively:

Cost = 0:

- Firm 1 Solar: 100 MW, Firm 3 Wind: 500 MW, Firm 4 Wind: 200 MW Total at cost 0 = 800 MW (cumulative 800)

Cost = 30:
- Firm 2 Gas: 500 MW Cumulative = 1,300 MW

Cost = 50:

- Firm 1 Coal: 100 MW Cumulative = 1,400 MW

Cost = 60:
- Firm 4 Coal: 400 MW, Firm 2 Coal: 200 MW Total 600 MW

Cumulative = 2,000 MW

## Page 2

Cost = 100:
- Firm 3 Oil: 100 MW

Cumulative = 2,100 MW

Thus the industry supply curve is a step function:

0-cost until 800 MW → 30-cost until 1,300 MW → 50-cost until 1,400 MW → 60-cost until 2,000 MW → 100-cost thereafter.

Supply will be 800 MW at $20 and 1300 MW at $40.

b) If demand is perfectly inelastic and equals 1,900 MW, what is the market price?

SOLUTION (B)

With perfectly inelastic demand at 1,900 MW, the price is set by the step of the supply curve where 1,900 MW falls. Capacity at cost 50 or below is 1,400 MW, which is not

enough. Capacity at cost 60 or below is 2,000 MW, which is enough. So the marginal plants are the 60-cost coal plants (Firm 2 Plant 4 and Firm 4 Plant 8), and the market price is $60/MWh. Of their 600 MW, only 500 MW is dispatched.

## Page 3

c) Given the market price you found in Part (b), compute the profit of Firm 1.

SOLUTION (C)

Profit = (Price – Variable Cost) × Output dispatched. Price = 60.

Firm 1:
- Solar (100 MW): (60 – 0) × 100 = 6,000

- Coal (100 MW): (60 – 50) × 100 = 1,000 Firm 1 profit = 7,000

d) Firm 1 considers adding 400 MW of new solar (VC = 0). How does Firm 1’s profit change?

SOLUTION (D)

New zero-cost capacity = 800 + 400 = 1,200 MW.

## Page 4

Even with the extra solar, meeting 1,900 MW of demand still requires using the 60-cost coal plants, so the market price remains 60.

Firm 1 output after the investment:
- Existing solar: 100 MW

- New solar: 400 MW
- Coal: 100 MW Total: 600 MW

Firm 1 profit after investment:
- 500 MW solar earn (60 – 0) × 500 = 30,000

- 100 MW coal earn (60 – 50) × 100 = 1,000 Total profit = 31,000

Original profit (from part c) = 7,000

Change in profit = 31,000 – 7,000 = +24,000

Firm 1’s total profit increases by 24,000.
