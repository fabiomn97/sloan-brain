Demand and Costs

1. Cross-price elasticity

Explain briefly.

Define the cross-price elasticity of demand. When is the cross-price elasticity of demand

positive vs. negative? (Hint: an example may help here.)

SOLUTION

The cross-price elasticity of demand (XED) measures how a change in the price of one good affects the quantity demanded of another good. The formula is:

XED = ​ %ΔP of Good B %ΔQ ​ of Good A D

Unlike the always negative price elasticity of demand, the value of the cross-price elasticity can be either negative or positive. When positive, the two goods are substitutes (like olive oil versus canola oil): if the price of olive oil goes up, demand will shift towards

canola, driving up the quantity of this good. When negative, the goods are complements (like pasta and pasta sauce): if the price of pasta goes up, consumers will demand less pasta and less sauce as well.

2. Why correlations miss demand

Explain briefly.

Briefly explain why correlations of price versus quantity are ineffective for estimating demand.

SOLUTION

Demand is a causal relationship: what happens to quantity demanded if we change price (all else equal). Correlations may capture both movements along and shifts in the demand curve and therefore be misleading.

4. Sunk acquisition cost

True, False, or Uncertain? Explain briefly.

You paid $1 million to acquire a new software startup, only to have a new competitor with a better product enter the market.

Your willingness to pay to acquire this competitor should be $1 million less than if you had not

done the first acquisition.

SOLUTION

False. The $1 million is sunk (it will not be recovered regardless of whether you acquire this competitor or not), so it should play no role in the decision. Your willingness to pay to acquire this competitor should be the extra profit you expect from owning it: its own expected profit plus any profit it would otherwise take from your existing startup. That

value may well differ from what it would have been without the first acquisition, but not because of the $1 million you already paid.

6. Own IT versus cloud

True, False, or Uncertain? Explain briefly.

Consider the choice between alternative information technologies (e.g., own IT department vs. Amazon Web Services).

Companies with high rates of return on their core business prefer technologies with higher upfront expenditures but lower variable costs in the longer run (e.g., own IT).

SOLUTION

False. If you can make a high return on your core business, your opportunity cost of

capital (the value of the best alternative foregone in taking that decision) is high, and you will prefer to save your money in the short run (and invest it) rather than face large startup costs. Cloud services like AWS (with higher variable costs but lower startup costs) are therefore more attractive.
