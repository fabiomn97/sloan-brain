OneSky should build the executive lounge, with a profit of $10,000. This can be achieved either by charging $250 and admitting both types, or by charging $350 and serving type

b) If there are 50 consumers of each type, but now OneSky can choose whether to have only an

From (a), the best single lounge earns $10,000. Because OneSky can split access by customer type, type H uses the executive lounge alone and type L uses the business

lounge alone, so each type can be charged its full "not shared" WTP: $350 for the executive lounge and $150 for the business lounge. Profits are 50 × (350 − 150) + 50 × (150 − 100) = 10,000 + 2,500 = $12,500. Hence it is better to offer both lounges

350 − 25 = $325. Profits would be 50 × (325 − 150) + 50 × (150 − 100) = $11,250, still above $10,000, so offering both lounges would remain the better choice.

4. 2023_midterm: Bakery cookies and macaroons

A local bakery is asking for your assistance, given your expertise. They are the only bakery in

town, but despite their monopoly status, they struggle. They submit the following question for your assessment:

The owners have been considering whether they should sell boxes of cookies, boxes of macaroons, or both. They worry that they will compete too much with each other. They commissioned a market survey revealing that 100 high-income consumers are willing to pay

up to $18 per box of macaroons and $6 per box of cookies. It also revealed that 200 Lowincome consumers are willing to pay $9 per box of macaroons and $3 per box of cookies. No consumer will buy both boxes or more than one. Producing a macaroon box costs $6 and producing a cookie costs $1. What is the recommended price for macaroons and cookies?

SOLUTION

They should set the price of cookies equal to the WTP of the low-types ($3) and then

compute the informational rents to the high type ($6 – $3 = $3, the surplus a high type would get from cookies) and subtract it from their WTP for the macaroons ($18 – $3 = $15). The prices are therefore $3 per cookie box and $15 per macaroon box.

Profit: cookies to 200 low types, (3 – 1) × 200 = $400; macaroons to 100 high types, (15 – 6) × 100 = $900. The total profit is $1,300.

(Check the alternatives: selling only macaroons to the high types at $18 gives (18 – 6) ×

100 = $1,200, and macaroons to everyone at $9 gives (9 – 6) × 300 = $900. Both are lower.)

5. 2023_midterm: adQT streaming plans

You operate a new on-demand video streaming service, adQT, and are contemplating what types of monthly membership plans to offer. You face two different types of customers, “spendthrifts” and “cheapskates,” both in equal numbers (100 spendthrifts and 100 cheapskates). Your marginal cost of providing streaming is zero, while the customer willingness to pay for different plan

options are given by the following table:

Ad-free Ad-lite Ad-heavy

Spendthrift $30 $23 $15

Cheapskate $15 $14 $11

For simplicity, assume that you receive no ad revenue so that the ads are a pure nuisance to your customers.

a) Suppose that you can segment the market and so directly sell one plan at a particular price to

the spendthrifts and similarly for the cheapskates. To maximize your profits, which plan would you sell and at what price to each of the market segments?


Given that ads are just a nuisance, there is no reason to include them. Instead, you just sell the ad-free plan to each segment and charge their willingness to pay for it. So you’d sell the Ad-free plan to the spendthrifts for $30/month, while selling the ad-free plan to

the cheapskates for $15/month

b) Suppose that you can no longer segment the market and need to rely on consumer selfselection, offering a menu of alternatives and allowing the consumers to choose which plan to pick. To maximize your profits, which two plans would you offer and at what prices?


It is clear that we still want the Spendthrifts to buy the ad-free plan, but we need to consider whether we want to introduce the Ad-lite or the Ad-heavy plans for the

Cheapskates. If we do Ad-lite, we can charge $14 for the plan to keep cheapskates buying, While the spendthrifts would get $9 of consumer surplus from the Ad-lite plan ($23-$14), so to keep them buying the Ad-free plan, we would need to price it at $21 ($30-$9). Revenue per pair of customers (one of each type) is then $14+$21=$35, so total

revenue is 100 × $35 = $3,500. If we do Ad-heavy, we can charge $11 for the plan to keep cheapskates buying, while spendthrifts would get $4 of CS from the Ad-heavy plan. Thus, we can price the Ad-free plan at $26 and still have the Spendthrifts to opt for the Ad-free plan. Revenue per pair is then $11+$26=$37, so total revenue is 100 × $37 = $3,700. Given

that here revenue equals profits, the optimal menu is an ad-free plan for $26 and an adheavy plan for $11.

## Page 8

c) Suppose that instead of equal numbers of spendthrifts and cheapskates, cheapskates outnumber spendthrifts 2 to 1 (there are 100 spendthrifts and 200 cheapskates). Would your

answer to part (b) change and briefly explain why or why not.


Yes, it would change. In part (b), switching from the ad-lite to ad-heavy plan cost you $3 per cheapskate customer but gained you $5 per spendthrift customer. But now if cheapskates outnumber spendthrifts 2 to 1, then the same gain of $5 on each spendthrift customer is now costing you $6 because of the higher consumption of the ad-supported

plan.

So you now offer Ad-free at $21 and Ad-lite at $14: profit = 100 × $21 + 200 × $14 =

$4,900, versus 100 × $26 + 200 × $11 = $4,800 with Ad-free and Ad-heavy.

d) Finally, suppose that you can receive ad revenue equal to $5 per customer in the Ad-lite plan and $10 per customer in the Ad-heavy plan. Would your answer to part (b) change and briefly explain why or why not.

SOLUTION (D)

The answer would not change. You are already providing the ad-heavy plan for the cheapskates so the extra ad revenue makes the plan even more attractive. For the

spendthrifts, on the other hand, the extra revenue you could get from the ads does not justify the decrease in the willingness to pay and you want to stick with the ad-free plan. To see this, note that spendthrift valuation decreases by $7 going from ad-free to ad-lite

and by $8 going from ad-lite to ad-heavy, which outweighs the $5 additional revenue that could be gained by each step.

Checking the menus with ad revenue included: Ad-free $26 + Ad-heavy $11 gives 100 × 26 + 100 × (11 + 10) = $4,700; Ad-free $21 + Ad-lite $14 gives 100 × 21 + 100 × (14 + 5) = $4,000; Ad-lite $19 + Ad-heavy $11 gives 100 × (19 + 5) + 100 × (11 + 10) = $4,500. The part (b) menu remains the best.

6. 2022_midterm: True/False/Uncertain

True, False, or Uncertain? Explain in 1–3 sentences.

a) In the monopoly setting, perfect price discrimination decreases deadweight loss compared to a single price for all consumers.


True. Under perfect price discrimination each unit is sold at its buyer's willingness to pay,
