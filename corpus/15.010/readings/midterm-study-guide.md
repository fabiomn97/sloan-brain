3. HOW TO STUDY

- The best way to study for this class is to practice doing problems. Work each one fully before looking at its solution, then redo the ones you missed a few days later.
- Focus on the logic behind each problem, not on memorizing answers: be able to say why, not just what.
- Revisit the in-class labs: they are the best guide to the kind of reasoning we test.
- Near the end, do a set of past questions under exam conditions: time yourself and use only your sheet of notes. Build that sheet as you study: formulas, the steps for each type of problem, and the traps you fell into.

4. TOPIC CHECKLIST AND WHERE TO PRACTICE

Past Exam L1-2-9; Practice: Demand and Costs; Recitation 1

CLASS YOU SHOULD BE ABLE TO PRACTICE 1. Consumer Demand Build market demand from willingness to pay; move between demand and inverse demand; tell a movement along the curve from a shift; compute elasticities and consumer surplus.

Past Exam L1-2-9; Practice: Demand and Costs; Recitation 1

2. Economic Costs Use opportunity cost and ignore sunk costs in decisions; separate fixed from variable cost; compute marginal and average cost; explain economies of scale and scope and their sources.

3. Supply & Perfect Competition

Past Exam L3-5; Practice: Perfect Competition and Market Supply

Read a supply curve as marginal cost; solve for the competitive equilibrium; predict how price and quantity respond when demand or supply shifts; explain entry, exit and long-run zero profit; compute the elasticity of supply.

4. Monopoly & Market Power

Past Exam L4; Practice: Monopoly Pricing and Market Power

Derive marginal revenue and set MR = MC; use the markup (inverse elasticity) rule; compute profit, consumer surplus and deadweight loss; explain the sources of market power, including network effects, where a buyer’s value depends on who else buys.

Past Exam L3-5

5. Electricity Markets Build a supply curve from plants ordered by marginal cost; find the price set by the marginal plant; work out how new capacity or demand changes price and profits.

Past Exam L6-7-8; Practice: Strategic Pricing; Recitation 2

6. Market Segmentation Set a different price in each segment using MR = MC or the markup rule; explain why the less elastic segment pays more; recognize when arbitrage breaks segmentation.

7. Self-Selection & Two-Part Tariffs

Past Exam L6-7-8; Practice: Strategic Pricing; Recitation 2

Price a two-part tariff (per-unit price at MC, fee equal to the binding type’s consumer surplus); compare plans for a consumer; check that each type picks the option meant for it.

8. Menus & Product Portfolios

Past Exam L6-7-8; Practice: Strategic Pricing; Recitation 2

Price a menu so the high type does not switch to the low version (upgrade pricing); decide which versions to offer, and when dropping the low version pays; spot cannibalization.

Past Exam L1-2-9 (demand estimation items)

9. Demand Estimation Explain why price and quantity data alone do not trace out a demand (or supply) curve, and why randomized experiments (or price changes unrelated to demand) identify it; explain perfect price discrimination.
