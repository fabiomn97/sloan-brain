12. 2018_midterm: Textbook and digital copy

A textbook publisher is a monopolist, because it holds a copyright. The individual demand curve for the book is given by

P(Q) = 20 −Q.

Therefore, marginal revenue is given by MR(Q) = 20 – 2Q.

The publisher has a constant marginal cost MC(Q) = 2, and zero fixed costs.

a) Calculate the quantity supplied by the monopolist, and the price. What are the total consumer surplus and the monopolist’s profit?

b) The publisher issues a digital copy of the book for verified students only.

The MC of producing a hard copy is still $2. However, the MC of producing a digital copy is $0 (also with zero fixed costs).

Assume that all students buy the digital copy and all professors buy the hard copy, i.e., the

publisher can perfectly segment the market.

The demand curves are given by:

Hard: P = 20 – Q (so MR=20 – 2Q)

Digital: P = 8 – Q (so MR=8 – 2Q)

What is the price and quantity sold in the hard copy market, as well as the price and quantity in the digital copy market?

c) What are the consumer surplus in the hard copy market, and the consumer surplus in the digital copy market?

d) Did the publication of a digital copy help any consumers? If so, which ones?

Q ​ = t 20 −P

and the demand function for each resident is

Q ​ = r 10 −P/2,

where Q is the number of rides demanded and P is the price per ride in dollars. There are 100 tourists and 100 residents. FunLand’s marginal and fixed costs are zero.

a) Suppose FunLand offers a single entrance fee and a zero price per ride. If FunLand wants to

sell to both types of consumers, what entrance fee should it charge to maximize profit? Can FunLand increase profit by selling only to tourists?

If FunLand wants residents to choose the “Value ticket” and tourists to choose the “Unlimited ticket”, what prices should it charge for these two types of tickets to maximize profit?
