CLASS 2 · IN-CLASS LAB · PART 1

The Costs of a Frontier AI Lab

In this part we look at the costs of an AI lab: which costs are fixed and which are variable, and whether there are sources of economies of scale and scope. The numbers are in the exhibits at the end; their sources follow the exhibits.

GROUPS Work in groups of 2 or 3.

THIS PART OpenAI (Exhibits 1 and 2): fixed or variable, then economies of scale and scope.

ANSWERS

Section B forms.gle/zLdch2MVuTnUhHav8 Section E forms.gle/44hJW5o4w4XkVtJX8

QUESTIONS

Exhibit 1 is OpenAI’s leaked income statement for 2025; Exhibit 2 shows the same lines for 2024 and 2025 side by side.

1. Which of the lines in the income statement in Exhibit 1 move with the number of tokens OpenAI

serves to its customers, and which do not?

2. Now focus on the compute expenses, lines 4 and 6. All of that compute is rented from Microsoft. Suppose the $6.05B of serving compute (line 4) is billed by the hour. Does that change your answer to question 1?

3. Now suppose it is a three-year contract at a fixed monthly amount. Does that change your answer? What decides whether a cost is fixed?

4. Exhibit 2 reports the income statement for 2024 and 2025. Which costs grew faster than revenue, and which grew slower? What does that tell you about economies of scale?

5. What are the sources of economies of scale here?

6. Does OpenAI benefit from economies of scope? If so, what are the sources of economies of scope here?
