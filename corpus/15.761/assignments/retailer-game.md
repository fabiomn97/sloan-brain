*Retailer Pricing Simulation Exercise (team)*

In class on December 1st, we'll be playing a simulation game that challenges you to think about markdown pricing strategy. The premise is simple. You are tasked with making pricing decisions for a product over 16 weeks. In each week, you observe that week's demand, remaining inventory, and total revenue. Your goal is to maximize your revenue over the entire sales season by strategically offering discounts.

You start with 2000 units of inventory, and the full price of the good is $100 each. For each week, you set the price for which there are four options: full price ($100), 10% discount ($90), 20% discount ($80), or 40% discount ($60). After you set the price for that particular week, you can click "**Step**" for the game to move on to the next week. You'll observe how many units you were able to sell during the past week. The graphs on the right half of the interface clearly show you how much inventory you have left, and how much revenue you have made.

There are a couple of things to note.

1) You cannot increase price. If you discount, say, 20%, then you can never go back to "full price" or a 10% discount.

2) You cannot replenish your inventory. If you run out, the game ends and you make no revenue in the remaining weeks.

3) You only have 90 seconds to make the pricing decision for each round.

We'll be playing this game live in class on December 1st. Everyone will be playing with the same "randomness" in the background, so it will be a fair competition between teams. In the meantime, you can begin to acquaint yourself with the game itself and the data provided may be useful.
