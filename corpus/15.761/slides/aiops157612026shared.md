## Slide 16 — Rule-Based Automation

- Many examples of intelligent processes that involve automation of simple (rule-based) tasks
- Extensive software development and sometimes using AI interpretation
- Many service industries (healthcare, food, retail, banking, airlines, etc)
- Benefits:Capacity, speed, scale, scarce human resources, structured and more reliable communication with customers
- Often interaction with other none-automated processes conducted by humans

## Slide 18 — ML-Enabled Automation

- The ‘next generation’ of intelligent processes involve automation of repeatable but more complex tasks (unstructured input & more complex rules, but structured output)
- Growing number of industries (Manufacturing, Insurance, retail, etc) – major impact on workforce and economics
- AI functionality – learning from prior examples (offline work) to implement automation, unstructured (more flexible) input but structured output (and consistent)
- Benefits:Capacity, speed, scale, scarce human resources, structured and more reliable communication
- Often (even more so) interaction with other none-automated processes conducted by humans

## Slide 19 — Large Language Models (LLMs)

- A technology (family of models) not a functionality
- Enable advanced search capabilities
- Enable next generation (semi) automation functionality (e.g., chat-box, coding, data extraction, information summary, generating content)
- Major issues related to transparency, interpretability & resilience

## Slide 25 — ML-Enabled Alert & Sensing

- New applications of intelligent processes that are based on far more refined sensing of the respective system to create situational awareness by human experts
- Leverage the fact that machines have far greater computational power and memory than humans and able to identify patterns (anomaly detection)
- Benefits:Inform experts to make better resource allocation and more timely decisions based on improve situational awareness
- Byproduct benefit is to better understand the system (more next)

## Slide 31 — What roles sensors serve?

- Understand the state of your system (e.g., machine sensors)
- Collect input signals to processes (what signals matters, what new signals could help – health wearables, Nauto)?
- Detect which customer is in front of you? More generally identify the type of process/rule you need to apply
- Measure the outcomes of your decisions
- Alert on changes or events

## Slide 32 — Types of sensors
