## Page 7

Risk Metrics vs. Decisions

-  Risk metrics are always chosen in the context of decisions and ‘business’ objectives and outcomes! Need metrics that you control!

-  Objectives:

– Minimize (COVID-19?) deaths (infections) and health harms – Ensure hospital systems will not collapse – Economic objectives/metrics

-  Interventions:

– Nonpharmaceutical interventions (‘lockdowns’, masks, testing, quarantine, etc) – Vaccines – Therapeutics (treatment) – Strengthening health systems (increase capacity)

Risk Metrics & Objectives

-  You manage only risks you measure (monitor)!

-  Managing short term vs. long terms risks is a major challenge

-  Need common metrics to compare between countries (apples to apples) otherwise almost hopeless

Measuring Risk Reduction by

Vaccines

-  Relative Risk Reduction (RRR) – Relative reduction of risk between vaccinated and unvaccinated individual:

Potential measurement biases (same population? testing differently?  background (baseline) risk? duration? variants?)

-  Absolute Risk Reduction (ARR) – Additive reduction of risk

-  Number Needed to Vaccinate (NNV = 1/ARR) – Number of vaccines needed to prevent 1 infection (severe case, death)

## Page 28

Measuring Vaccine Risks

-  Risks of vaccines are hard to measure (short-long terms, lack of controlled environment beyond RCT, background noise)

-  Require extensive monitoring and investigation to detect weak signals

-  Need robust reporting systems and good understanding of background noise

-  ‘Acceptable safety’ is defined with respect to risks (likelihood & severity of outcomes) and the alternatives (risk of not taking it – natural immunity, treatment, etc)

## Page 29

Measuring Vaccine Risks

-  When analyzing risk tradeoff need to make sure you compare the same population (risk disaggregation)

-  One natural metric is all-cause mortality and serious medical events

-  Other risks metrics relate to long term impact of repeated vaccination
