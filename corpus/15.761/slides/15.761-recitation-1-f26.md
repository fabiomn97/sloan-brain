## Page 8

Background

- Littlefield Technologies produce satellite systems upon requests

from customers (under some contract)

- Delivery on time generates revenue

- Your task is to manage the operations of the factory (over the

next 180 simulated days)

- The goal is to generate as much cash as possible until the end of

the simulation by honoring customers’ contracts

## Page 9

Contracts

Each incoming customer/job order will correspond to a contract

All contracts have the same terms:

-  Littlefield must deliver 60 satellite systems, also called a

“kit”, within 48 hrs

-  The amount received per contract is $25,000

-  If the delivery is delayed, the price is discounted at a rate of

$20,000 per day

-  This means that if delivery takes place after more than 78

hours, it does not generate revenue

## Page 11

The Production Line – Part 1

-  Once an order is received, raw material for 60 satellite systems are

automatically retrieved from the warehouse

You don’t have control over the inventory of materials

-  The materials are then divided into lots (e.g., 1 lot of 60 kits, 2 lots of

30 kits, etc.)

-  Lots travel through the production line independently, until all of them

are ready to ship

-  Lots need to go through 4 stages of production

-  Stages: (1) Board stuffing, (2) Testing, (3) Tuning, (4) Final Testing

-  Set-up time = incurred once per lot, irrespective of lot size

-  Operation time = incurred per unit/ kit within the lot

-  Average processing time of each stage is known:

## Page 15

The Stations

- Board stuffing (for stage 1), Testing (for stages 2, 4), Tuning (for

stage 3)

- Stations 1, 3 currently have 10 machines installed, station 2 has

15 installed

- Once a lot reaches a station, it is either processed (if a machine

is idle) or queued

- All stations have FIFO policies; Testing station can prioritize over

jobs of stage 2 or 4 (you decide!)

## Page 23

Decision 2 – Changing Lot Size

- Change lot size from
- 60 (1 lot/job), 30 (2 lots/job), 20 (3 lots/job), 12 (5 lots/job), or 6 (10 lots/job)

- Smaller lot size allows you to break up order’s production over multiple machines at once
- Lot size of 20 breaks up over 3 machines
- Lot size of 6 breaks up over 10 machines

- Smaller lot sizes mean
- More setup time (i.e., downtime)
- Faster Cycle Time (if you have enough capacity)

Vocabulary:
- 1 Customer Order = 60 units ordered by a customer
- 1 Customer Order = 1 Factory Job = 60 kits
- 1 lot = batch size (between 6 & 60 kits)
