Course Syllabus 15.002 Leadership Challenges for an Inclusive World 1 unit, P/D/F

About 15.002 At MIT Sloan, our mission is to nurture principled, innovative leaders dedicated to making a positive impact on the world. Recent global events have underscored the pressing need for transformative leadership capable of addressing the challenges we face as a society.

15.002 LEAD Week: Leadership Challenges for an Inclusive World gives first-semester MBA students a core educational experience focused on fostering effective leadership, paired with a front-row seat to the innovation happening right outside your classroom an opportunity unique to the MIT ecosystem: connecting with MIT's Presidential Initiatives. The course is grounded in the belief that successful leadership requires a deep understanding of human complexity in our interconnected world.

Over three days, students select from 30 highly interactive workshops spanning leadership, ethics, professional development, and innovation, and build a personalized schedule of six workshops—two per day—through LEAD bidding. Along the way, students will explore the MIT Presidential Initiatives, dig into their own values and leadership style, and build lifelong skills for a successful career in a dynamic global market.

Workshops are organized across three days, each central to MIT Sloan's mission of developing principled, innovative leaders:

-  Day 1: Monday, October 26 — Leading Yourself & Leading with Others. Self-Awareness & Purpose: Explore your values, motivations, and identity to build the foundation for authentic leadership. Collaborative Leadership: Develop the skills to build trust, communicate effectively, and lead within teams and diverse groups.
-  Day 2: Tuesday, October 27 — Professional Development & MIT Presidential Initiatives. Sharpen your value proposition and networking skills—including how to leverage LinkedIn and AI tools. Explore MIT's labs and companies for a firsthand look at the research and innovation behind the Institute's Presidential Initiatives, from climate to health to advanced manufacturing. It's a chance to deepen your connection to MIT and spark further engagement, whether through research, coursework, or a startup spinning out of these initiatives.
-  Day 3: Wednesday, October 28 — Leading with Integrity. Ethical & Impactful Leadership: Learn to navigate complexity and ambiguity with integrity, make decisions that reflect your values, and create positive change.

To accommodate varied schedules, many workshops are offered twice daily, in both morning and afternoon sessions.

Defining Your Ethical Boundaries of Tolerance: Where Should We Draw the Line on AI? (AM only)

Jeffery Saviano, Senior Lecturer, AI Strategy, Governance, and Ethics

Session Description: Artificial intelligence is advancing faster than the rules meant to govern it. Most organizations are racing ahead without a clear governance framework for what is acceptable, and what is not, when deploying powerful AI systems. This workshop asks you to confront the toughest leadership question of our time: Where do you draw the line?

Building on original research at Harvard and tested in executive workshops, Jeffrey Saviano introduces the Boundaries of Tolerance Framework, a governance model that translates philosophical principles of AI ethics into actionable guardrails for society, business, and leadership. Through interactive scenarios, participants will debate live cases of algorithmic bias, ethical trade-offs, and fiduciary duty, discovering how different tolerance levels can lead to radically different outcomes.

This is not a theoretical seminar. It is an unflinching look at the governance choices leaders face when AI systems challenge fairness, safety, and trust.

By the end, you will have practiced defining your own boundaries of tolerance, explored how to operationalize them, and considered the risks of failing to act. Along the way, we will grapple with pressing questions such as: What ethical boundaries should never be crossed in AI deployment? How can leaders balance speed, profit, and responsibility under pressure? Who should decide when AI risk is “acceptable,” and by what criteria?

Expect a provocative, high-energy session that goes beyond compliance to challenge your assumptions about innovation, governance, and leadership in the age of intelligent machines.

By the end of this workshop, participants will be able to:

-  Identify and articulate their own ethical boundaries of tolerance when evaluating AI risks.
-  Understand how philosophical principles of AI ethics can be translated into a practical governance framework for business leaders.
-  Apply the Boundaries of Tolerance Framework to real-world scenarios involving bias, ethical trade-offs, human oversight, and fiduciary responsibility.

Exploring the Self: Techniques for Mindful Leadership in the Age of AI

Cameron Cler, MIT Sloan MBA ’20

Session Description: How do you lead with clarity when your attention is under siege? This session offers a proactive approach to mindfulness and stress management on your path to becoming a better leader. You will leave with practical tools you can start using immediately and build on throughout your career: techniques for burnout prevention, emotion regulation, and greater self-awareness across your personal and professional selves.

We will work through three connected practice areas. First, mindfulness for mental wellbeing and stress management: the science behind self-regulation as a leadership skill, plus guided breathwork and meditation you can adapt to a demanding schedule. Second, boundaries for better work-life balance: you will identify your natural boundary style (porous, rigid, or balanced) and practice values-based boundary conversations through partner role play. Third, deep work for enhanced focus: a systematic four-step cycle (intention, grounding, flow state, reflection) that channels mindfulness into the sustained concentration that fosters better leaders, teams, and organizations.

New this year, we will explore how AI fits into a mindful leadership practice. Each module includes hands-on prompts for turning an AI assistant into a reflection partner: a mindfulness coach, a boundary-setting advisor, and a deep work debrief partner. We will also discuss the flip side: protecting your attention and human presence as AI accelerates the pace and volume of work.

This session is timely for first-year MBAs: seven weeks into Core is exactly when stress patterns form, and the habits you set now will shape how you lead under pressure.

The approach is highly experiential: guided practice, partner dialogue and role play, silent reflection, live AI exercises, and discussion, including examples from Sloan. The process will also emerge in response to the interest and energy of the group.

By the end of this workshop, participants will be able to:

-  Understand self-regulation as a foundational, evidence-backed leadership capacity

-  Practice concrete tools for stress management (breathwork, meditation, journaling) and commit to one practice for the rest of Core

-  Identify your default boundary style (porous, rigid, or balanced) and rehearse a values-based boundary conversation

-  Design a personal deep work cycle (intention, grounding, flow state, reflection) and share it with a peer for accountability

-  Leave with a set of AI reflection prompts to sustain each practice, and a clearer view of how to use AI to protect rather than fragment their attention

Giving and Receiving Feedback: A Relational Approach to Leadership

Lucia Kamm, Founder & Partner, Watershed Coaching

Session Description: For most of us, feedback does not feel like information. It feels like a verdict. A note on a slide deck can feel like a note on our competence, a comment on how we showed up in a meeting can feel like a comment on whether we belong. These reactions are normal, especially among high achievers used to being evaluated and used to doing well. This session starts from this reality, not around it.

## Page 14

In a small, confidential group, you will practice the hardest and most useful techniques in feedback: separating what happened from what it means about you as a person. You will work hands on with the 5-What's model (Intention, Behavior, Impact, What I Know About You, What's Next) for feedback that's direct without being harsh, and with MIT Leadership Center's principles of relational feedback: dialogue over judgment, cultural awareness, and psychological safety. This is especially relevant given roughly 42% of this cohort brings different cultural norms around directness.

This session is highly experiential: most of the time is spent in paired practice, not lecture, and nothing here requires you to already be good at this. You will workshop real feedback you want to give, get coached by a peer, and leave with one concrete thing to try this week, not a checklist.

By the end, you will distinguish behavior from interpretation, apply a repeatable framework for feedback that preserves relationships, and identify your real barriers to receiving feedback well, without it meaning something is wrong with you.

Feedback fluency is foundational to leading teams and building trust with new colleagues. There's no lower stakes place to practice than with the people you will be alongside at MIT.

By the end of the session participants will be able to:

-  Distinguish observable behavior from interpretation and assumption

-  Apply the 5-What's model to deliver direct, evidence based, relationship preserving feedback

-  Practice receiving feedback without becoming defensive

-  Recognize personal barriers (cultural, emotional, or habitual) to giving and receiving feedback well

Leadership and the Inner Critic: An Experiential Leadership Lab

Daena Giardella, Senior Lecturer, MIT Sloan School of Management, Organizational Consultant for Leadership, Teaming, and Communication Development, Executive Leadership Coach, and Faculty Affiliate MIT Leadership Center

Session Description: Our ability to be effective in our “outer” negotiations or conversations with other stakeholders depends greatly on our ability to adeptly manage and navigate our own “Internal Negotiation” with our Inner Critic. This capability is crucial for leaders who wish to be skillful influencers —especially during difficult conversations. Managing one’s internal negotiation is one of the key aspects of emotional self-regulation in leadership, which is the ability to regulate one’s emotions and reactions as we respond to situations throughout the day when we are faced with stress, conflict, difficult conversations, disappointments, etc. Emotional self-regulation is the ability to respond spontaneously with ease and relational awareness when needed as well as the ability to avoid counterproductive, impulsive, or reflexive reactions. Internally and externally conflictive situations that take us out of our comfort zone can often stir up our Inner Critic, which is defined as “Negative Self-Talk”— toxic, self-condemning thoughts, insecurities, beliefs, and emotions, which cause self-doubt and diminish our confidence, freedom to take risks, and trust in our choices. It sabotages our ability to be ourselves authentically. Effectively managing these intrusive thoughts helps us to be spontaneous in the moment, creative, and collaborative.

## Page 15

Sometimes our Inner Critic messages arise from conditioned family or peer group early messages, internalized unconscious bias, negative stereotypes, or social prejudices that affect how we feel about ourselves. Many times, one’s Inner Critic messages translate into a feeling of “Imposter Syndrome,” which can cause us to doubt our achievements, question our intelligence in comparison to others, or secretly worry that we could be exposed as a “fraud” who doesn’t belong where we are. The skillful leader is aware of this universal inner obstacle and has reliable methods for successfully managing it in oneself and helping others manage it as well. This awareness is a crucial dimension of our “emotional intelligence” skill set as a leader that helps cultivate psychological safety, individual development, creativity, and optimal execution in a team.

The concept of the Inner Critic as well as its impact on leadership and team communication will be explored. Students will learn how to identify this experience and manage it successfully with a specific set of skills and self-reflection practices. We will also discuss how understanding the universality of the Inner Critic can help leaders and team members be more effective and strategic as they collaborate, communicate, negotiate, mentor, and influence others.

Requirements: This workshop is a highly experiential leadership learning lab that will incorporate skill practice and frameworks as well as some Improvisational Leadership techniques and other interactive exercises. Please bring a journal and pen. Participants are asked to be present for the entire session with prompt arrival. If you have an extenuating circumstance and need to arrive late or leave early, please contact me in advance of the workshop, so we can discuss together whether it is possible for you to attend.

Listening to Lead: Making a Difference Through Transformative Listening

Andy Cahill, Executive Coach, MIT Leadership Center

Session Description: Successful, sustainable teams in every arena - sports, theater, military, corporate, startup, etc – all share certain qualities of trust, collaboration, and a seemingly effortless exchange of power, creativity, and complimentary skills.

Great leaders recognize how valuable this cohesion is. They work to unlock it by modeling a listening practice that simultaneously empowers each unique individual while also encouraging everyone to understand and contribute to the bigger picture.

In this interactive 3-hour workshop, we’ll unpack the fundamentals of deep listening, exploring what it looks and feels like to move from a “transactional” to a “transformational” listening stance, leveraging individual reflection, peer dialogue, and large group experiential exercises to get you practicing right away.

We’ll also spend some time at the end thinking about how you can start leveraging these listening skills right now, in your MBA experience, to improve the quality of your peer connections, increase networking opportunities, and enhance learning retention across your coursework. By the end of the session students will be able to:
