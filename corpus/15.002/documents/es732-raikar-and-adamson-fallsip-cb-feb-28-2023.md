COURSE INTRODUCTION:

Renewable energy is now a major industry, attracting over $450 billion in capital investment in 2019. Yet, renewable energy is still only a small part of the energy mix in the United States and most other countries. The transition to a carbon-sustainable global economy will require trillions of dollars in capital investment by 2030. But how can capital-intensive renewable energy projects attract the needed capital? Without massive private investment, plans for a green energy future in the developed and developing world will remain as aspirations. Harnessing the capital markets to build out global renewable energy infrastructure is critical.

In this course we will examine how large-scale renewable energy projects actually get financed and built, using the structures of modern non-recourse project finance. We will examine the economics of large-scale green power production and incentive mechanisms, and how they fit into the U.S. and global energy mix. Through a mixture of lectures and real-life examples students will get to see how renewable energy project finance works in actual deals that mix finance, public policy, legal, engineering, and environmental issues. The skills developed in analyzing non-recourse cash flow-based finance will be generally applicable to students interested not just in green energy, but also more widely in private equity and infrastructure investing.

COURSE OBJECTIVES:

The course is designed to introduce students to the project finance practice as applied to the renewable energy sector, complementing academic finance theory with the practical aspects of financing capital intensive energy projects. The course will employ spreadsheet projects to explore the structures used in project finance. The course will also discuss how financing requirements affect technology choice and public policy in the renewable energy sectors. The course is designed for students with an interest in the green energy sector, ESG investing and debt and private equity finance, with an orientation towards the practical application of the concepts in real-world transactions.

STUDENT LEARNING OBJECTIVES:

As a result of this course students should be able to:

Understand the economics of renewable energy in the U.S. and global energy mix, and the incentive mechanisms to enable rapid deployment of renewable energy projects for a sustainable energy mix.

Learn the basic concepts of non-recourse project finance, and how project developers, lenders and other deal participants evaluate investments in these projects.

Develop financial models in Excel of transaction structures (equity, tax-equity, debt) and how these are used to allocate and manage risks by various investors.
