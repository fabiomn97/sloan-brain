## Page 2

SEE BIAS: Here are some potential barriers to fair and effective talent conversations

Higher bar: Due to stereotypes, certain individuals can be held to a higher standard. Sounds like extra scrutiny.

New criteria: Introducing previously undiscussed criteria into the process that may justify a “gut” reaction

Work-life penalties: Talking about parental or caregiving status and assumed (negative) impact on career goals

Criticism of communication style that is not specified in job description and is not crucial to job performance

Unwritten rules: Using criteria that are not defined or that only “insiders” can know or meet

Vague praise: Offering praise that lacks specific impact, e.g. “She had a great year”

Overvaluing agentic traits (stereotyped as male) such as assertiveness, self-sufficiency, dominance

Undervaluing communal traits (stereotyped as female) such as supportiveness, interpersonal sensitivity

BLOCK BIAS: Learn and practice strategies to support fair and effective talent conversations Agree to importance of criteria “Let’s agree to focus on our top criteria. Here is what we decided upon…”

“Let’s include  _______ in our evaluation.” [Prior to evaluation]

Broaden criteria (in advance, as necessary)

Reference original criteria “While ______ may be worth considering, we agreed _____ was the most important criteria.” Block use of new criteria “We agreed [original criteria] was most important. Let’s focus on that in this round.” Reference unused criteria “We haven’t discussed ______, which we identified as our top criterion. Can we take a moment to do that now?” Disregard ineffective or biased criteria “That doesn’t seem to be helping us make the decision.  Let’s stick to …”

“Hmmm…would that be an issue if _________ had behaved in a similar way?”

Ask for clarification “Could you say more about why _____ is important in this evaluation” Ask if a similar comment would be made about someone with a different identity
