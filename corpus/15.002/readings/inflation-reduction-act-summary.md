Production Tax Credit (PTC)

Increased back to 100% of the stated PTC rate with potential for bonus adders through the end of 2024 once Prevailing Wage and Apprenticeship requirements are met

> PTC Base Rate $15/MWh subject to inflation adjustment (currently at $26/MWh for 2022)

> Headline Credit = Base Credit (20% of Headline Credit) + Bonus Credit (80% of Headline Credit)

Two-Tier Credit System

> Solar projects can elect for PTCs in lieu of ITC

> Clean-Hydrogen projects can avail PTC for the first 10 years of operation, but these type of

projects will not qualify for “domestic content” bonus adder (regardless of PTC/ITC election)

> Laborers are paid prevailing wages during construction of a project and repairs (only during

relevant tax credit period)

> Applicable percentage of total labor hours for the construction of the project are performed by

Wage and Apprenticeship Requirements

qualified apprentices; 12.5% in 2023 and 15% in 2024 onwards

> Cure mechanism to rectify any non-compliance

> Bonus Credit available only if certain wage and apprenticeship conditions are met. However,

projects that begin construction prior to the date that is 60 days after the IRS guidance and projects under 1 Mwac net output are exempt from the wage and apprenticeship requirements

Other Important Information

> Similar to the ITC, PTCs are also eligible for bonus adders in the form of additional “cents-per-

KWh” increase equivalent to 10% adder but do not qualify for any low-income adders

Direct Pay

Direct Pay allows certain tax-exempt entities to receive an amount in direct payment equal to certain tax credits generated

> Applicable entities include:

- Any tax-exempt entity, State or local government, Tennessee Valley Authority, Indian Tribal Government, or Alaskan Native Corporation

> Provisions specific to the PTC:

- Availability only for the applicable entities with projects placed in service after December 31, 2022

- Election must be made no later than the due date for the tax return in which the election is made

- “Election” refers to the 10-year period beginning on the date when the facility is placed in service

> Other important general provisions:

- Entities eligible for direct pay are not eligible to transfer (sell) credits

- Exception from “tax-exempt” requirements for first 5 years of the clean hydrogen credit, the first five years of the 45Q credit, or the section 45X advanced manufacturing credit allows any taxpayer to elect direct pay

## Page 13

Transferability Provisions

Beginning in 2023, Tax Credits can now be sold for cash to Unrelated Third-Parties

> Only current year credits can be sold, but the “buyer” of credits may carry the tax credit backwards

> Tax credits that are generated after December 31, 2022, and sold are now eligible for three-year

carryback

> Tax credits that have been transferred are still subject to recapture under section 50(a)1

> For smaller projects (under 7 MWDC), transfer of tax credits may result in greater tax credit

monetization with potential for further increase in value from an FMW step-up2

> While tax credits may only be transferred once, a taxpayer can sell original credits to more than one

transferee

- Proceeds from the sale of tax credits will not be subject to federal taxation and cost of credits are not deductible

Green Hydrogen Storage

Hydrogen Storage can qualify for a tax credit and adders under Section 48 ITC for “energy storage technology”

> Requirements for full-ITC @ 30% include:

- Property which receives, stores, or delivers energy for conversion electricity, and has a nameplate capacity of not less than 5 kilowatt hours of storage

- Available to projects placed in service beginning in 2023

- Subject to prevailing wage and apprenticeship requirements

> Like Solar and Wind structures, standalone hydrogen storage technology is eligible for the same
