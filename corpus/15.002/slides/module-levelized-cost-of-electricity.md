## Page 2

Power versus Energy

- Power = rate of doing work (creating energy)

- Basic unit equals the Watt (1 joule/sec) – kind of small

- For electricity, we use kilowatts (kW) and megawatts (MW) for power and capacity

- Capacity = maximum power that a plant can generate at any point

- Energy = Power  * time

- Example – if a plant generates 10 MW of power for 5 hours, it will generate 50 MWh

- Dimensional analysis

## Page 3

Capacity factor

- Capacity Factor = A fraction expressed as the energy generated over a period divided by

the amount of energy that could be generated over the same period if the plant ran at full capacity

- Example 1: Plant of 100 MW that produces 350,000 MWh in a year. What is the capacity

factor:

- # Hours in a Year = 24 * 365 = 8,760
- Plant could produce 100 MW * 8760 hours = 876,000 MWh if it ran at full capacity for the whole

year [8700 hours = 24 hours/day * 365 days]
- Capacity factor = 350,000/876,000 = 0.4 (or 40%)

- Example 2: A wind farm with an installed capacity of 200 MW has a capacity factor of

0.35. How much does it generate in a year
- Energy = Capacity * Capacity factor * # of hours
- Energy = 200 MW * 0.35 * 8760 hours ~=  613,200 MWh

## Page 4

Energy Yield

- Energy Yield = A fraction expressed as the energy generated over a period divided by the

capacity of the project

- Example 1: Plant of 100 MW that produces 350,000 MWh in a year. What is the Energy

Yield:

- Energy Yield = 350,000/100 = 3,500

- Example 2: A solar farm with an installed capacity of 200 MW has a Annual Energy Yield

of 1,800. How much does it generate in a year

- Energy = Capacity * Energy Yield
- Energy = 200 MW * 1,800 =  360,000 MWh

- Trivia: How would you calculate Capacity Factor from Energy Yield?

## Page 5

Developing Pro-forma Projections

- Example 1: A wind farm with an installed capacity of 200 MW has a capacity factor of

35%. What are the expected revenues if the project has a PPA in place at $20/MWh?
- Energy = Capacity * Capacity factor * # of hours
- Energy = 200 MW * 0.35 * 8760 hours =  613,200 MWh
- Annual Revenues = $12,264,000

- Example 2: If the aforementioned wind farm has an O&M expense of $45/KW-year what

is the annual O&M expense? What is the EBITDA for the Project?

- O&M Expenses = O&M Expense in $/KW-year * Capacity of the Project in MW * 1,000
- O&M Expenses = $45/KW-year * 200 MW * 1,000 KW/MW= $9,000,000/year
- EBITDA = Revenues – O&M Costs
- EBITDA = $12,264,000 - $9,000,000 = $3,264,000

- Example 3: If the project costs $1,500 per KW to build, what is the total capital cost?

- Capital Cost = Per Unit Capital Cost * Capacity of the Project * 1,000
- Capital Cost = 1,500 * 200 MW * 1,000 = $300,000,000

## Page 6

Levelized Cost of Electricity (LCOE)

- LCOE is an economic measure to compare the lifetime costs of electricity from various generation technologies

- Up-front costs
- O&M Costs
- Disposition Costs
- Useful Life

- LCOE can also be viewed as an “average” electricity price a specific generation source should yield for breakeven

- LCOEs are primarily used as a “relative” scale to compare various technologies rather than as an

“absolute” measure to make investment decisions

- LCOE Calculators
- Lazard Annual Survey of LCOE
- NREL LCOE Calculator (https://www.nrel.gov/analysis/tech-lcoe.html)
