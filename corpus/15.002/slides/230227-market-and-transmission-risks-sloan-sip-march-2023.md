## Page 9

Energy Market Dynamics

o The ISO energy markets procure electricity to meet consumers’ demands both in real time and in the near term, which includes:

-  The sale or purchase of energy in Real-Time Energy Market (often occurring every five minutes)
- The sale or purchase of energy in Day-Ahead Energy Market (occurring one day forward)

o The clearing price for electricity in the wholesale market is determined by an auction in which various generating resources offer in a price at which they can supply a certain amount of energy

o These offers form a supply curve, seen on the following slide, which sets the price of electricity

## Page 10

Locational Marginal Pricing

- The  major US markets are LMP based
- Creates hourly (and sub-hourly RT) prices for electricity at every location
- LMP = energy cost + marginal congestion costs + marginal losses

## Page 11

LMP pricing creates locational price basis

o The generator is always paid the LMP at its location (=$30/MWh in this example)

o The load pays the LMP at its location on the grid ((=$40/MWh in this example)

o The difference in prices is called “basis”

o The locational basis can be change every hour and can be very volatile

Hedging congestion price risks

o Locational pricing creates basis risks for purchasers and sellers of power

o Someone bears this risk – depending on where the power is sold under contract

o If sold at the project interconnection node – the buyer holds the transmission risk o If sold at a hub point or elsewhere – project holds risk from interconnection node to sales point o Need a way to hedge and price these risks

Financial transmission rights

o Locational hedging contracts sold in auctions by ISOs

o Act as a hedge for the congestion component of LMPs

o Financial swap for a set amount of capacity over a period (a month, six months, a year, etc.)

o Does not affect physical operations – pays out whether or not the plant is running

Market and Transmission Risks

FTR payoff structure

o FTR payoff structure designed to offset the congestion cost due from sale of power at different points on the grid

o In the sale transaction, generator is paid source node price, and load pays sink node price

o FTR pays the difference between the sink and source node congestion prices times a fixed quantity

o If the quantities match up this offers a hedge for the congestion price difference

## Page 24

Market and Transmission Risks

Transmission risks over the long term

o FTRs may not hedge the congestion price risks if the FTR quantity does not match the output (common with renewables)

o FTRs are usually sold for relatively short time horizons (e.g. up to 1 or 2 years) – not life of a new project

o Need to account for long-term transmission risks if project is not hedged

o Utilities have better ability to secure long-term transmission service and manage these risks
