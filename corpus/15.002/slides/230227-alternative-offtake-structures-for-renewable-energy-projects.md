Evolution of the PPA as an Offtake Strategy

- Utility PPA provides the most attractive offtake strategy
- Utilities have excellent credit profile
- Utilities are willing to buy electricity at the point of interconnection and can provide longer term

PPAs (15 – 30 yars)
- Utilities traditionally have had lenient credit support requirements from the projects

- Unfortunately, several utilities have met or are close to meet their RPS requirements. Therefore, unless the states revise the RPS standards, they don’t have any incentives to buy renewable energy through PPAs

- Corporates have stepped in to fill the void created by the Utilities
- Inexpensive power for data center applications
- CSR/ESG mandates

- Corporate PPAs (also called Virtual PPAs) are helpful but there can be structuring issues for developers/lenders

- Often shorter term (10 – 15 years)
- Basis Risks – Delivery point at a liquid hub not at the generator
- Stringent credit requirements
- Limited price protection: e.g. may have collar structure or limited protection in negative price

Basic Renewable Energy Hedge Structure

- A forward start hedge that is put into place when a Notice to Proceed (NTP) is issued
- Typically, concurrently with construction financing and tax equity commitment
- The hedge has a delayed start to ensure that the Project Company doesn’t face any “naked”

exposure if there are any construction delays

- The hedge is “shaped” using on-peak and off-peak blocks
- Part science, part art

- Typically, hedges are executed at a liquid hub
- The project is exposed to basis differential, which can be addressed separately

- Firm LD v/s Unit-contingent hedges
- The Project Company doesn’t face liquidated damages if there are unforeseen weather events

- There is a cushion between the Project’s expected outcome and the hedged volumes
- Hedges are executed based on 1-year P-99 or 1-year P-95 volume (residual merchant exposure)

- Various forms of hedge arrangements
- Physical hedge
- Financial Swap
- Contract for Difference

- Project gets paid/pays amount H * (P-LMP) – when LMP is lower than P the project gets paid the difference, and when LMP is high tit pays the difference to give a fixed price for the hedge volume H

## Page 11

Non unit-contingent Commodity hedge – simple hourly examples

- Example 1 – Low LMP
- Hedge volume (H) in an hour is 60 MWh at hedge price P = $25/MWh
- In hour 7 in a day, output Q is 80 MWh and prevailing LMP is $20/MWh
- Project company gets paid $1600 (80 MWh * $20/MWh) for sales into ISO market
- Hedge payoff with bank = H * (P – LMP) = 60 MWh *  ($25/MWh - $20/MWh) = $300 to project

company (since hedge price was higher than LMP) – net hourly project revenue = $1900

- Example 2 – Low LMP
- Hedge volume (H) in an hour is 60 MWh at hedge price P = $25/MWh
- In hour 12 in a day, output Q is 70 MWh and prevailing LMP is $45/MWh
- Project company gets paid $3150 (70 MWh * $45/MWh) for sales into ISO market
- Hedge payoff with bank = H * (P – LMP) = 60 MWh *  ($25/MWh - $45/MWh) = -$1200 from

project company (since LMP > hedge price)
- Net hourly project revenue = $3150 -$1200 = $1950

- Example 3 – High LMP and zero output
- Hedge volume (H) in an hour is 60 MWh at hedge price P = $25/MWh
- In hour 16 in a day, output Q is 0 MWh and prevailing LMP is $1000/MWh
- Project company gets paid $0 for sales into ISO market
- Hedge payoff = H * (P – LMP) = 60 MWh *  ($25/MWh - $1000/MWh) = -$58,500 from project

company

## Page 12

Issues in Structuring Energy Hedges for Renewable Energy Projects

- Most of the projects are owned by a single purpose entity and financed using nonrecourse debt or equity

- Construction cannot begin without revenue certainty and a hedge provider is exposed to construction risk until the project is commercially operational

- Forward starting hedges are costly especially if construction period is lengthy
- Liquidity is the primary constraint
- Less of an issue for renewable energy projects with a shorter construction period

- Intermittent nature of the resource makes the hedge structuring difficult

- Basis differential pose additional constraints

- Options are very expensive

- Traditional liquid credit support alternative is not available because Sponsors typically have limited access to capital or balance sheet on their own. Lien based structures have issues of their own

- Forbearance if tax equity is required
- Inter-creditor matters

## Page 13

Tracking Account

- Tracking Account is a useful concept to manage the residual risk for the Project Company
- Works like a working capital facility. The basic idea is that while it is hard to predict how much

wind production will be in a short interval (e.g. an hour), there is a much better predictability over a longer time period (e.g. year). Therefore, as long as we don’t hedge 100%, the residual merchant exposure should smooth out variability in energy production and basis differential because dollars are fungible!

- At the inception, the Tracking Account starts at zero
- Every dollar that the Project Company earns in excess of the hedged revenues increases the

Tracking Account balance (i.e. makes Tracking Account more positive)
- Every dollar that the Project Company owes due to revenue differential arising from non-delivery

of the guaranteed volumes (e.g. 1-year P-99), shaping differences (i.e. producing electricity at times different from what was agreed upon), outages, etc. reduces the Tracking Account balance (i.e. makes Tracking Account makes more negative)
- There is a periodic accounting for what went into Tracking Account and what was taken out.

Generally bounded by zero and a negative number (negative number signifies draw on the Tracking Account)

- Systemic risks can make the Tracking Account go awry. Also, it is not a leverage facility for dividends but working capital facility for revenue smoothing

## Page 14

Credit Risks Associated with Hedge Structures

- There is a correlation between commodities prices and credit risk.
- Think about an oil producer selling oil forward or an airline company buying oil forward. As the oil

prices increase the credit quality of an oil producer increases
- The correlation holds better for producers than consumers. For example, for airline companies fuel

is one of many variables that contribute to its profitability
- The correlation depends on credit spreads. Well capitalized highly rated companies don’t exhibit

any correlation
- The correlation for natural gas power producers is more difficult to quantify. A power producer is

essentially earning the Spark Spread. Therefore, if the natural gas is on margin  in a given market, the credit correlation doesn’t hold as well for a natural gas producer
- However, with no fuel costs, wind and solar projects are a different story

- Potential Pitfall – Operational Risks – which cannot be priced accurately

- It’s difficult to hedge correlation. Therefore, while there could be a meaningful pricing advantage, CVA traders wouldn’t price the correlation benefits

- However, right way risk can be exploited to create interesting structured products

## Page 16

Proxy Revenue Swap (PRS)

- A weather derivative Product offered by insurance companies
- Allianz and Munich Re are the major players

- Similar to a fixed-for-floating swap but the Swap is based on P-50 production irrespective of the actual electricity production

- The P-50 production is based on the designed “S-Curve” for the wind turbine model

- Pros
- Eliminates residual merchant production – typically, the financial swaps are struck at 1-year P-99

level, leaving the Project to merchant price risk for 10 – 20% of the volume
- The collateral requirements are light – no liens, just LCs

- Cons
- The P-50 production is based on a manufacturer specified “S-Curve” with a tolerance band

exposing the Project Company to curve mismatch risk
- Doesn’t cover basis risk
- Term can be limited to 10 years whereas typical financial swaps can go as far as 13 years

- Example Transactions: Bloom Wind Project (Capital Power), Cotton Plains Wind, Old Settler, and Phantom Solar (Apex)

## Page 29

Merchant Price Risk Exposure

- Merchant price risks arise for multiple reasons

- Offtake contracts may cover only a portion of the expected output from a renewable energy output

(e.g. commodities hedges cover only 1-year P-99 level output, which typically covers 75 – 90% of the expected generation of the renewable energy facility)

- As PPAs are becoming shorter and shorter, the renewable energy projects are increasingly

exposed to merchant price risk

- Nowadays, both utilities PPA and Corporate PPAs have a term of 10 – 15 years

- Commodities hedges with banks have a term of 10 – 15 years

- Proxy Revenue Swaps have a term of 10 – 12 years

- To the extent, a project receives revenues at a price other than the point of interconnection, the

project is exposed to the transmission basis exposure

- Therefore, it is important that the commodities merchant price exposure is understood as a part of investment underwriting process

- Project developers and financial institutions rely on price curves supplied by reputable sources

- Alternatively, the financing parties may engage an independent market consultant to develop

market price projections
